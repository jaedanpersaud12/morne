# 14. Writing rules (locked)

These are the rules every chapter follows, so we never rewrite the book to fix them later. If a rule changes, change it here first.

## Prose

- Close third person, past tense. One point-of-view character per chapter, named in the first line.
- Vary the rhythm. A longer sentence carries the action, a short one lands it. Three short sentences in a row only when something has just broken. Plain words.
- No "and then, and then" chains. One action per sentence, or a clean list with commas. Never "he did this, and the X did that, and the Y happened."
- No simile unless it changes what the reader sees. If it sounds like writing, rewrite it.
- No filter words (saw, heard, felt, noticed, realised, thought) unless the noticing is the point. The thing just happens.
- The narrator never explains what a moment means. If the POV character wouldn't think it, it isn't on the page.
- "Said" only. No adverbs on it.
- Dialogue in light island English: "is the dust", "allyuh", "we going down the beach". Readable by anyone, never phonetic spelling.
- Nothing hidden from the reader for style. If the POV character knows it, the reader knows it.

## Chapters

- About 3,000 words. Three scenes, separated by a rule: set up, complicate, turn.
- Every scene has a goal, something in the way, and ends worse than it started.
- The POV character gets one Sequel beat per chapter: feel it, weigh it, decide. The decision becomes the next chapter's goal.
- Every chapter ends on a turn (a reveal, a threat, a question), never a mood. The reader must already know enough for the turn to land; a hook that depends on information they don't have is confusion, not suspense.
- Chapter titles are one word. No countdown headers.

## Characters

- Introduce at most two or three people per chapter, through what they do, never through a description paragraph.
- A character gets a physical or personality detail only when the scene needs it. No "he was always eating something" unless the eating is doing work right then.
- Backstory comes out in pieces, under pressure, to another character, or when something in the room triggers it. Never to the reader directly, never in a paragraph that stops the scene. Nobody narrates their family history mid-panic.
- Reactions come in order: gut, reflex, then words. Never skip the feeling.
- Depth tiers hold. See [cast overview](cast/_overview.md#depth-tiers-locked).
- Not every trait needs a wound. Some people are funny because they are funny.

## Shock, then drive

- The collapse lands as shock first: real panic, real freezing, somebody being sick. Nobody is calm about it. Juno's calm is a performance she pays for in private.
- Then drive, immediately. The characters think ahead on the first night, not in chapter twenty: water for the sleepers, babies in cribs nobody is feeding, the hospital, old people alone, the little ones. They act on it the same night.
- The story rewards thinking ahead and punishes not doing it. When someone forgets a crib, we see what that costs.
- The Trace thinks about the whole island because that is what the theme says a community is. Looking only after your own street is the thing the Trace will later be tempted into, not where it starts.
- Denial is real. People say "they'll wake up" and "someone will come by morning." Juno lets them, and plans anyway.

## Grounded

- Before anything with an engine runs, somebody found fuel. Before anybody eats, somebody caught, grew, traded or stole it. See [13](13-resources-and-roles.md).
- Powers arrive like puberty: uneven, scary, embarrassing, uncontrolled.
- Every power costs. If the chapter skips the cost, add it back.
- Science explains the mechanism, never the meaning.

## The group's private language

The six are so close their humour makes no sense to anyone else. Use it:

- **Let an outsider be the lens.** Kit watches them laugh at something incomprehensible and feels shut out. The reader feels it with her. Slowly she learns the jokes, and so does the reader. By the end, the reader is in the group.
- **Never explain a joke on the spot.** Let a reference float by. Two hundred pages later we learn where it came from. (The goat is not even a goat.)
- **Make it a survival tool.** Over a radio the Harbour is listening to, the group can say things only they understand.
- **Use it to measure damage.** When they stop joking, the reader knows things are bad. When one of them dies, a joke dies too. The first time someone makes it again is the moment the group starts to heal.

## Writing suicide and depression

Never show method details. Focus on the before and after: the feeling of being cornered, and the slow work of coming back. Recovery is not one moment; Fen and Juno still have bad days. Never use it as a twist or a shock; reveal it the way a real friend would tell you. Keep hope visible: both of them are proof that people come back from the edge.

## Writing Sol's story

The fear of being cut off is real and specific to where she grew up. Let the reader sit in the risk with her before anyone's answer comes. Acceptance from the group should cost something and mean something, not arrive for free.

## Combat

- Powers on the page do work before they do damage. If a chapter has Tock burn someone, it also has him weld something that morning.
- A power is thirty seconds. Fights are short. Anything longer is cutlasses and bats and running.
- Nobody is a superhero. No fireballs, no flight, no force fields. The twins are the only pair who look like a movie, and only together.
- Guns end arguments. The Harbour has them. Write every Trace fight as a fight they are trying not to have.
- Injuries last weeks and go to Juno. A fight in chapter twelve is a wound in chapter fifteen.
- The first time a kid kills with a power, something goes quiet in them for days. The network noticed.
- Crews are weather. The protagonists see smoke, a price, a face at the gate. They rarely see the cause.

## Added 2026-11-02 (from the blind read of ch 1 to 8)

### Decisions cost
- Every chapter's turn is a decision that **costs the POV character something they chose over something else**, and the book makes them pay for it on the page within three chapters. "Chose Granny over Mr. Hale, but he coughed less" is cover, not cost. "Took Judge's twelve bags knowing what a gift from him means" is cost. If the chapter gives the character an out, cut the out.
- Fen's one-more-day (ch 6) is the model: a kind decision with a real bill. The bill arrives (Juno hears about the ship from Judge, not Fen) before the kindness has time to feel like wisdom.

### Sleepers become people, once a chapter
- One beat per chapter where a sleeper is briefly a person again: a cough that sounds like any morning, hands that open, a first name off a bill. No explanation, then back to the rows. See [19](19-imagery-and-threads.md#granny-dot-the-present-absence).

### The corbeaux attend every death
- Before anyone dies, the birds come down. Nobody remarks on it. See [19](19-imagery-and-threads.md#three-imagery-systems-keep-all-three-alive).

### Dialect is class
- The Trace kids speak lighter dialect than the Harbour. That is deliberate and it is a fault line (Judge: "You're shorter than the paper"). Ambrose and Tock carry the most dialect on the hill; Juno the least, because she's been correcting herself for a scholarship since Form One. Never flatten it by accident; when a Trace kid drops into heavier dialect, something is wrong with them.

### Titles
- One word, a concrete noun the chapter can be held by (Dust, Spoon, Market). Numbers only when the number is the object (Sixteen). Element words (Ice, Heat) tell the reader the structure; don't give every powered kid an element title. Lonny's chapter is not "Earth." Kit's is not "Rot."

### Tics to count before saving a version
Run these on LATEST.md and halve whatever is over:
- `and ... and ... and` chains in quiet paragraphs (keep them for panic only)
- "the way X did" / "the way Y would have" (one per scene at most)
- "Nobody said anything." / "Nobody moved." (one per chapter)
- Section endings that land on a one-line punch (let one section per chapter just stop)
- Clinical terms the POV circles without naming (one per chapter; three in a row is a mannerism)
- A character narrating their own mistake right after the reader has felt it ("he had said her name... because having an answer had felt like being useful"). Trust the reader once.
- Juno's glasses (pushed up, smudged, in her hair): one per chapter. Give her face another anchor.
- Lonny eating: he eats, and it matters, and the seed is loud. A third fewer mentions until the kitchen chapter where someone says it out loud.
