# 20. Theories and big-picture logic

Added 2026-11-02, after the second review pass. Part 1 is what the characters *think* is happening, so the mystery always has something on the table. Part 2 is where the bible and the chapters don't add up yet. Everything here is **PROPOSED** for Jaedan to accept, change or strike.

## Part 1: the theory ladder

**The rule:** a mystery with no guesses is just a list of events. The kids should always have a theory on the table, and it should be wrong in a way the reader can see a little before the characters do. A theory dies when somebody tests it, never when the narrator says so. Each death leaves one true piece behind.

### What the reader holds by ch 8

1. Adults sleep. Kids don't.
2. It came on a Sunday, in dust season, out of season.
3. Kids are getting powers, and each one matches what already marked the kid.
4. A ship is enforcing a quarantine, and it knows about the hill.
5. Mrs. Quill holds at 94% on room air.
6. White threads in the grave soil.
7. The church standpipe still runs.
8. Being kept on your feet delays the Sleep by hours.

### How it plays across the book

Think of it as a staircase where every step is a little closer to the truth, and every step costs something to climb:
- **Movement 1–2:** dust, water and Sunday die fast. Plasticity becomes the working belief.
- **Movement 3:** "they did it" (Fen) runs against "the ground is taking them" (Kit and Moth). This mirrors the beds-or-the-ground split: Juno's side reads the outside world, Kit's side reads the soil.
- **Movement 4:** the radio kills "they did it." "It's catching" is confirmed. The quarry story comes back, and nobody laughs this time.

### Experiments the kids can actually run

They're clever, and clever kids test things. Each of these is a scene, not a paragraph:
- **Waking.** Smelling salts and cold water from Dr. Moss's cupboard. Juno considers his adrenaline and doesn't use it, because she can't undo it. Nothing works, and the fact that nothing works is data.
- **Age.** Juno's exercise book gets a new column: ages of everyone asleep and everyone awake. The line isn't clean (Part 2, item 2). She starts writing her own birthday at the top of pages.
- **Place.** Count powers by street. The Trace's rate is three times town's. Fen maps it.
- **Time.** Ambrose lasted until 4:45 because Kit kept him moving. Kit knows this before anyone, and nobody asks her.

## Part 2: big-picture logic

### 1. Granny lives a year in a bed. The bible says beds last weeks.

- **The problem:** [04](04-sleepers.md) says feeding is impossible without a tube: "weeks, not months." The outline still has Granny failing in a bed in ch 29, about month eleven, and Ida's father alive in a bed at the end. Both can't be true.
- **PROPOSED fix: tube feeding, bought from the Harbour.** The hospital store Judge raided also has feeding tubes and enteral formula. Juno learns nasogastric feeding from the book in movement 2. It's dangerous (a tube in the lung kills), slow, and needs supplies only Judge has.
- **Why it's better than a patch:** every week Granny stays in a bed is a week Juno owes Judge. It's what gives the "gift" in ch 8 its price, and the reason the Trace can't walk away from the Harbour. Granny still declines (tube feeding by a sixteen-year-old with a book isn't hospital care), so ch 29 stands.
- **Where:** first raised by Juno in ch 10 ("they're wasting; the book says tube"). The second gift in ch 13 is the tubes.

### 2. The age line has no teenagers asleep on the page.

- **The problem:** bible [02](02-the-sleep.md) says roughly 16–19 is a coin flip: some sleep on day zero, some fade. On the page, every teenager we meet is awake: Wren (17), Judge, Nell, the six. The scariest fact in the bible, that nobody knows where their edge is, has no body on the page.
- **PROPOSED fix: one borderline sleeper the reader knows.** A 17- or 18-year-old on the road or at the Harbour who fell asleep on day zero with the adults. Options:
  - Wren's older sister, in the bed next to their mother.
  - One of Judge's boat crew.
  - A Trace kid in Juno's year, which hits hardest.
- **Why it matters:** this is where the theory ladder and the age clock meet. Juno (the oldest, no power) sees someone her age in the rows, and her birthday becomes a clock from that page on.
- **Where:** ch 9 or 10, by name.

### 3. Nobody tries to talk to the ship.

- **The problem:** Judge transmits on a handheld VHF every night. The ship broadcasts on channel 16. The first thing any kid with a radio would do is call the ship back. Also, Juno's father in Toronto is awake, so the outside world knows these children exist. Bible 02 says supply drops happen for a few weeks, but none are on the page by day 8.
- **PROPOSED fix (two pieces):**
  - **Ch 6 or 9:** Judge, or Fen through Judge, hails the ship on 16. The answer is the same recorded loop, every time. Make it a recording, not silence, so the reader sees a decision was made somewhere: the world is talking *at* them, not *to* them. Light imagery: being seen and not answered.
  - **Movement 2:** the first supply drop, about week 2. Formula, ORS sachets, a satellite phone that only dials one number. Who gets to the pallet first is a fight, and the Harbour wins. Drops stop by week 5, without explanation.

### 4. Where are the other four thousand town kids?

- **The problem:** Morne Bay has about 15,000 people, which is roughly 4,500 kids. Judge has 310.
- **PROPOSED answer:** most stayed home with their sleepers. Judge's 310 are the ones with nobody: kids whose sleepers died, kids from the boats, kids too young to turn an adult. One line from Nell in ch 8 or 13: "The rest are in their houses. We go round." It also explains why bed deaths in town are invisible to the Trace for weeks.

### 5. Babies, island-wide.

- **The problem:** about 20,000 people means a few hundred babies under one, most breastfed, with mothers asleep. The formula count is the first mass death on the island and the book only shows three babies.
- **PROPOSED:** this isn't a hole, it's an off-page horror the reader should feel once. The children's house at the Harbour (ch 8) is mostly babies. Nell's ledger has a formula column that only goes down. Goats for milk ([13](13-resources-and-roles.md)) become the Hills' first real currency.

### 6. Judge needs Juno, out of 310 kids.

- **The problem:** this holds, but only just. Out of 310 kids, nobody else knows first aid?
- **PROPOSED:** let Nell say the Harbour had two kids who'd done Red Cross courses, and one is asleep. That's the borderline line again (item 2). The other is twelve. One line, and Judge's need stops looking convenient.

### 7. The Trace's water, light and fuel are bright targets by day 8, and only the Harbour notices.

- **The problem:** fine for now. Ears explains the Harbour. But the Junction boys have watched every trip past the gas station, and a lit house on a black island draws everyone.
- **PROPOSED:** no change before ch 12. The outline already sends the Junction crew up the road then. Just make sure ch 9–11 have one sign that the Junction is counting too: a boy at the bend at night, or a missing jerrycan.

## Quick test for every new chapter

Before saving a version, ask three questions:
1. **What theory is on the table?** If none, the mystery is standing still.
2. **What did somebody pay for?** Rule 14, decisions cost.
3. **Which off-page faction moved?** The parallel-stories grid in [17](17-book-one-outline.md#parallel-stories-the-world-off-page).
