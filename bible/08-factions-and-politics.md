# 08. Factions and politics

Factions form around **ideas**, never around types of people. Each one is an ecosystem: fighters, healers, builders, cooks, powered and powerless, all mixed. A healthy forest needs decomposers, pollinators, predators and shade plants, and so does a faction. The drama comes from people in the wrong faction.

## The four

| Faction | Belief | Has | Lacks | Drift over time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Trace | Protect what we have and hold until rescue | A hill, one road in, medicine, water, a generator, a medic | Food, numbers, fuel | From neighbourhood to fort; from "protect" to "keep out" |
| The Harbour | Order through strength | Fish, boats, warehouses, the gas station, the hospital stores, the most kids | Medicine know-how, clean water, trust | From the island's only government to a food-controlled regime |
| The Hills | The island isn't the enemy | Crops, land knowledge, the truth about the network | Defences, medicine | From quiet farmers to something like a faith |
| The Shore | Every kid for themselves, or get off the island | Salvage, rumours from the ships | Everything else | Small, unpredictable, the island's unreliable newspaper |

## The fifth group: nobody

About a hundred kids by month six who won't join anyone: crews of three to eight, raiders, kids with ridiculous powers and nothing to do with them. They are not a faction. They are the island's weather. The Junction crew below the hill, the Mas Camp on the coast road, the Gas Truck, the Lighthouse. Full list in [07](07-powers-in-practice.md#the-unaligned-crews-raiders-and-kids-with-ridiculous-powers).

## Firepower

| | Powered | Fighters | Guns | What that means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trace | 8 | 4 | 1 | Can't win a fight. Can refuse to have one: a hill, a wall, a welded gate, the twins. |
| Harbour | about 120 | about 30 | 20 to 25 | Can take anything on the island by force except the hill, cheaply. |
| Hills | about 130 | a handful | a few | Won't fight. Roots what dies there. |
| Crews | about 15 | all of them | 3 or 4 | Can't hold anything. Can take anything that isn't guarded tonight. |

The Trace's politics follow from that table: it survives by being worth more alive (Juno's hands, Sol's ice) than it would cost to take. See [07](07-powers-in-practice.md#combat).

## The three questions every faction splits on

1. **Wait or build?** Is rescue coming, or is nobody coming and we need a real society?
2. **Who leads?** Should powered kids rule because they can protect everyone, or is that the start of a hierarchy?
3. **Medicine or the island?** Keep sleepers alive with dwindling supplies, or give them to the earth?

## Resources are the real politics

See [13](13-resources-and-roles.md) for the full ledger. The short version:

- **Food.** Powers burn calories, so whoever controls food controls who can fight. The Harbour holds the fish. The Hills hold the crops. The Trace holds neither, which is its weakness.
- **Fuel.** The Harbour holds the gas station and the fishing fleet's diesel. Without fuel, no generators, no boats, no trucks. The Trace's generators die in weeks without a deal.
- **Ice.** [Sol](cast/sol.md) can preserve food and medicine. Every faction wants her.
- **Medicine.** The Trace's cupboard. The hospital in town, which the Harbour holds and nobody knows how to use.
- **Fresh water.** The Trace's tanks and the quarry pond versus the Harbour's dead mains and the Hills' streams.
- **Information.** [Fen](cast/fen.md)'s radio. He knows what the outside world is saying before anyone else, and he's the only line to Judge.

## How power shifts over three years

1. **Year one: scramble.** Every group hoards. The Trace blocks its road. [Judge](cast/judge.md) organises food when nobody else will and becomes the closest thing to government. The children's house at the fish market is the first thing he's admired for, and his first lever.
2. **Year two: trade and pressure.** The Trace's medicine runs low and it must trade with the Harbour for fish and fuel. The Hills' rooted sleepers survive while others die, and kids start going north. The Harbour starts taxing. Judge and [the Gardener](cast/gardener.md) make their deal: the strongman and the saint.
3. **Year three: the question of the sleepers.** Something changes in the rooted adults. The Hills know first. The island's politics stop being about food and become about what the sleepers are becoming, and who gets to decide.

## How small things move the world

Write these as ripples, not events. The protagonists see smoke, a price, a face at the gate; they rarely see the cause.

- A generator running all night on the hill tells the whole island who has fuel. (Night one.)
- One box of antibiotics traded to the Harbour sets the price of medicine for a year.
- Sol freezing a fish hold for Judge makes her the most valuable person on the island and puts a target on the Trace.
- A rooted sleeper living when a bedridden one dies empties a faction.
- A boat sunk at the quarantine line ends the "rescue is coming" faction overnight.
- The day the Trace turns away healthy kids in the dry season, the road calls it cruelty. The reader has seen the tanks, and knows it isn't.

## The door: who the Trace takes in, and where it stops

Decided 2026-11-03. **The Trace are not bad people.** They take in the sick. They work hard to build a life inside the walls that can hold more people than it could yesterday. When they say no, they are doing what they have to do, and it costs them.

**The wall exists so the door can stay open.** A lifeboat has a number painted on the side, and people keep swimming toward it. The Trace's answer is not to stop pulling people in; it is to keep making the boat bigger. Every sustainable thing they build is a way to say yes one more time:

| What they build | What it lets them say yes to |
| --- | --- |
| The turning grid and the ward (week 1) | Every sleeper on the road, not just their own |
| The Nettle girl's well (month 3) | The dry season without sending anyone away for longer |
| The antibiotics-for-fish trade (month 3) | Protein for thirty, and medicine for the Harbour's sick |
| Sol's ice for the fish hold (month 4) | A week's food from a day's fish |
| Tock's wall and the welded gate (month 9) | Keeping the door open by day without being overrun |
| The quarry-floor garden (month 9) | A future that doesn't depend on Judge |

**The line.** They stop when taking one more would kill someone already inside. That is never written down as a rule. It shows up as a number in Juno's exercise book and a face at the gate. Juno is the one who most wants to take everyone, because she is the doctor, and she is also the one who counts. That is her conflict in one fact.

**What never changes:** the sick come in. Every time, all year. Little ones come in ([12](12-little-ones.md)).

**How the door moves over book one:**

| When | The door | Where it's on the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Month 1 | Anyone. Wren and Pip, the wheelbarrow kids, the kids at the bend, Cass and his mother. | Ch 2, 4, 5, 11 |
| Months 2–3 | Anyone. Newcomers are asked to bring water or hands. They take in a hurt boy from town without a question. | Ch 13 (Adrian) |
| Month 5 | The boy they took in claims the place. They put him out: the first time the Trace ever turns someone out, and it's someone it took in. From now on every stranger is looked at first, and the looking costs (a kid with a fever waits at the gate while someone checks her). | Ch 17 |
| Months 4–6, deep dry | The limit. The sick still come in. Healthy kids are turned away until the rain, with water, directions to the Hills and their names remembered. Juno says no herself so nobody else has to. | Ch 19 |
| Months 7–8 | The rain comes back and the door opens wider again. | Ch 21 |
| Months 9–10, the fort | The gate shut at night, open by day. The sick still come in. | Ch 24 |
| After the siege | They treat the dock boy whose face Sol froze and send him home with a note. The door stays open because they choose it, and now they know the price. | Ch 25, 27 |

**Who stands where:** Lonny says yes too easily and pays for it with his own body. Juno counts, and pays from her own ration first. Cass asks who decides. [Adrian](cast/adrian.md) is the reason kindness becomes dangerous. None of the six is ever the villain of a door decision; if a scene makes one of them look cruel without showing the arithmetic, the scene is wrong.

## Leadership inside the Trace

**Nobody leads.** That is the point, and the theme. The Trace runs on domains, each held by the friend who can hold it:

| Domain | Who | Why it's theirs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sleepers and medicine | [Juno](cast/juno.md) | The books, the table in her head |
| The gate, the wall, the runs outside | [Tock](cast/tock.md) and [Lonny](cast/lonny.md) | Heat and muscle; they are the ones who go out |
| Power, radio, Judge | [Fen](cast/fen.md) | The generator, the handheld, the only line to town |
| The little ones and morale | [Lyra](cast/lyra.md) | Loud, patient, impossible to ignore |
| Food and cold | [Sol](cast/sol.md) | Ice turns a day's fish into a week's food |
| The ground, the Hills, Moth | [Kit](cast/kit.md) | The only one who hears it |

Decisions that cross domains get made in the Vales' kitchen, by the six, arguing. The goat is how they argue.

**The pressure from outside is that someone must be in charge.** Judge wants a name to deal with. The kids at the bend ask for the doctor. Cass and the older boys ask why one friend group decides for thirty kids, and the honest answer is that nobody else was in the kitchen on night one. Everyone lands on Juno because she was in the paper. She keeps trying to carry it. The friends keep taking it off her. That tension is Juno's arc and the Trace's politics at the same time.

**Cass's complaint is sharper than "why her":** *why them?* Six friends who were a group before the Sleep now run a road of thirty, and the other twenty-four never chose it. He is right about that, which is why the Gardener gets to him first.
