# 17. Book one outline

**One line:** a girl who has spent her life earning the right to be loved tries to keep her grandmother alive through a year where the island keeps offering her an easier way, while her friends keep refusing to let her do it alone.

**Spine:** the sleepers. Beds or the ground. Juno's medicine is the old world; Kit's garden is the new one. The book is Juno losing that argument slowly, one bed death at a time, while being right about everything else.

**Length:** about 30 chapters, 3,000 words each, around 90,000 words. POV rotates among [Juno](cast/juno.md), [Fen](cast/fen.md) and [Kit](cast/kit.md); the other friends get one chapter each when something happens to them; short interludes from Judge and the Hills from movement 3 on.

## Proposed insertions (2026-11-02)

From the blind read of ch 1 to 8. Each is **PROPOSED**: beat-sheeted so it can be written in Jaedan's voice or struck. Where one replaces an existing row, the row it replaces is named.

### 6½ (or the first scene of 9) — Kit — "Box"

**Why:** Kit opens the book and then is a hand lifted from a gallery for seven chapters. Eight chapters is too long to park the opening POV; the steel box at the end of ch 8 shows the book knows she matters. One scene inside her head before ch 9 keeps her a person, not a Chekhov's gun.

**When:** day 6, the evening after the funeral, while Fen is on the roof (ch 6) and Tock is scrubbing the counter (ch 7). About 1,200 words; a half-chapter or the cold open of ch 9.

| Beat | What happens | Cost / seed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Kit wants to keep Ambrose on the gallery and off Dr. Moss's floor, where the rows are. She has not said why. | The reader learns why: the ward is where the road's people are, and she is not the road's. The flat is the only place she has ever been inside first. |
| Obstacle | Juno sends Lonny down with a mattress and the turning schedule. Lonny is kind about it, which is worse. | Lonny says "you're one of us" and she doesn't believe him and doesn't argue, because he carried her father's weight once already. |

### 9 and 10 — the theory, voiced and wrong

**Why:** by ch 8 the reader holds four pieces (adults sleep, kids don't, powers, quarantine) and no character has tried to fit two together. A mystery with no hypothesis is a list of events. The bible already says the kids believe brain plasticity for most of book one; somebody has to say it first, and somebody has to say the wrong thing before them.

- **Ch 9 (Kit):** Ida, over the ward, says it was Sunday. Everybody who fell was in church or had the service on. "God put them down to listen." The Nettle girl believes it instantly. Kit, whose father had the service on the car radio, doesn't, and can't say why, and the ground under the flat is warm.
- **Ch 10 (Juno):** Juno answers with the nursing book: a chapter on adolescent brains still wiring until the mid-twenties. "It got into something that's still being built. It couldn't get into something finished." She is half right ([02](02-the-sleep.md)) and says it like it's whole. Fen, listening, thinks about the ship and says nothing. Tock asks the only good question: "Then what does it do to the finished ones?" Nobody answers.
- Rule from here: one theory per movement, each one wrong in a way the reader can see before the characters do.

### 7 — Tock's pot, off the page (revision note, not an insertion)

Tock gets two revelations in one chapter (the pot, then the tile) plus a demonstration (the table). The tile is where Ida enters and must stay. The table is where Juno answers and must stay. The pot is the weakest of the three. **PROPOSED revision:** open ch 7 on the melted handles already on the stove and the handprint already on the counter, Tock scrubbing, the fever three days old. The reader arrives after the first time, the way Ida does. Saves about 600 words and makes the tile the first on-page fire.

### Side arcs under the main plot

- **Lonny on the road** (ch 17 to 26): off-page for nine chapters. Keeping twelve houses alive with his hands. The Junction learns not to cross him. The girl's mother dies in her bed anyway. He comes back for Bix's funeral and never says what the road was like.
- **Ambrose in the ground**: rooted in the yard at the bend, slowly changing. Every Kit chapter checks on him in one line. By movement 5 his eyes are open and track nothing, and the Gardener says he's dreaming of her.
- **The Quill parents**: their mother in the garden by ch 18. Their father still in a bed in the hall, whom the twins never decided anything about. The sleeper nobody talks about.
- **Kit's mother**: a Morne Bay number that rang out. In movement 3 Nell mentions a woman asleep in a flat on the coast road with Kit's surname on the mailbox. Kit doesn't go. Book two.
- **Cass**: from "why do six friends decide" to a hardened kid in the gate to the Gardener's man inside the walls. His mother's bed death is the hinge. The reader sees every step and agrees with half of them.
- **Ida**: never roots her father. By ch 29 hers is the last bed in the Trace, and her father is alive. The counter-argument the book never resolves.
- **Juno's father in Toronto**: awake, and the outside world knows where his children are. A voicemail in ch 2, a name the scientist says on the channel in ch 22.
- **Marsh**: fresh water from the sea, kidneys as the price. The Harbour's answer to the dry season, and the first person on the island to be used up by her gift on purpose, by someone who knew.
- **Big Hob**: eighteen, fading. What the age line looks like from the outside, a year before Juno reaches it.
