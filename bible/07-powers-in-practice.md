# 07. Powers in practice: numbers, daily use, combat, and the unaligned

The power system in [03](03-power-system.md) says what powers are. This file says what people do with them: how many kids have them, what they're for on a Tuesday, what happens when they fight, and what the island looks like once desperate kids with strange gifts stop belonging to anyone.

**Why the Trace has so many.** One in ten is the island rate. Eight of twenty-two is more than one in three. Quarry Trace sits on the quarry wound where the network's roots are densest ([05](05-network-and-gardener.md#why-now)). The Hills, over the deep roots, run high too. The Harbour runs low (one in ten) but has ten times the kids, so in absolute terms it has fifteen times the Trace's powered population. **The Trace is never the strongest faction. It is the most concentrated.**

**The eight in the Trace:** [Tock](cast/tock.md), [Lonny](cast/lonny.md), [Fen](cast/fen.md), [Lyra](cast/lyra.md), [Sol](cast/sol.md), [Kit](cast/kit.md), Cass (awakens month three: Earth, a hardening of the skin, which makes him harder to argue with and easier for the Gardener to use), and the Nettle girl, 12 (month five: a small Water gift, she can find water underground by standing still, which is the most useful power in the dry season and belongs to a child).

**Awakening rate over time.** Month one: 3 in the Trace (Sol, Tock, Lonny). Month three: 6. Month six: 8. After that, only the second bloom changes it.

## Powers on a Tuesday

The rule from [03](03-power-system.md): powers are for moments, not for living. Thirty seconds of real output, then shaking and hunger. But thirty seconds, every day, changes everything. Most power use in the book is mundane, and the mundane uses are what make the Trace work.

**The Harbour's roster** (names in [supporting](cast/supporting.md#the-harbours-powered-kids)): Judge (the sea), Dax and three battery kids (Lightning), Marsh (fresh water from seawater, at the cost of her kidneys), Big Hob (Earth, 18, fading), Cookie (Fire, feeds two hundred), Ears (Air, the lookout). No healers.

**What the Trace doesn't have, and the Harbour does:** Lightning. A Lightning kid can jump-start a dead engine, charge a car battery by holding the terminals, and run a radio off their own body. Judge has four of them, which is why the Harbour has power and the Trace has one generator and a shrinking fuel can ([13](13-resources-and-roles.md#fuel)). The Trace's long game is solar; the Harbour's is Dax.

**Healers.** Juno has no power. The Hills have two Life/decay kids who can take pain out of a person and into themselves, which is not healing, but looks like it, and is what the Hills' faith grows from. The Trace's medicine is books and hands. That asymmetry is a trade route and a temptation.

**Mechanics.** No power replaces knowing how an engine works. Fen learns from manuals and from the two garage apprentices at the Harbour, who teach him in exchange for ice. A Lightning kid can start a car; only a mechanic can keep it running.

## Combat

Nobody is a superhero. Tock can weld; he cannot throw fireballs. Fights are short, ugly, and mostly fought with the island's actual weapons.

### The rules

1. **A power is thirty seconds.** Then shaking, hunger, and in Tock's case a fever. A fight that lasts longer than that is cutlasses and bats.
2. **Most fighting is ordinary.** Cutlasses (every yard has one), cricket bats, bottles, car bonnets as shields, Carnival costume pieces as armour (chest plates, headdress wire, and it is not a joke when you're looking at it). One powered kid per side, maybe two.
3. **Guns change everything, and the Harbour has them.** Judge took the police station in week one. Forty guns on the whole island and he holds more than half. The Trace has one shotgun and eleven shells. Every fight with the Harbour is a fight the Trace can only win by not having it.
4. **Terrain and value beat force.** The Trace survives because it's on a hill behind a wall they built themselves and a gate Tock welded, and because Judge needs Juno's hands and Sol's ice more than he needs the hill. The day that stops being true is the siege.
5. **Killing with a power costs more than the kid expects.** The network notices. The first time a powered kid kills someone, something in them goes quiet for days, and later that kid's power may turn up in someone else. See [03](03-power-system.md#rules-we-can-use-later) on echoes.
6. **Injuries last.** A cutlass wound in year one is weeks of Juno's work and a real chance of dying of infection. Nobody walks it off.
7. **The twins are the exception,** and the reason the Trace is feared out of proportion to its size. Together they are the best fighters on the island, because the harder you hit them the stronger they get. Alone, each is beatable, which everyone eventually works out.

## The unaligned: crews, raiders, and kids with ridiculous powers

Between the factions is everyone who won't join one. On a small island that is maybe a hundred kids by month six, in a dozen crews of three to eight, and everyone knows their names, because everyone went to school with them.

**How they form.** A house empties (the sleepers die, or get rooted, or the kids just leave). Two or three kids with nothing to lose find a car with petrol in it. By month three they've learned that a kid with a strange gift can walk into a house and take what's there, and nobody comes. By month six some of them have stopped pretending it's survival.

**What they want.** Food, batteries, fuel, medicine, and by year two, each other. They don't want territory. They want to not be told what to do, which is the one thing every faction is offering.

**The island version of Mad Max.** No desert, no highways. Instead: cars run until the petrol goes bad, then bicycles and the gas truck, which one crew keeps alive for a year. Carnival costume pieces as armour. Dogs, which the crews feed and the factions don't. Graffiti on the coast road: a crew's name on every house they've emptied. A kid in a welding mask who never takes it off. Cricket pads. A boy who rides the roof of the gas truck with a cutlass and a Lightning girl beside him keeping its battery alive.

### How crews touch the factions

- **The Trace** sits above the Junction. The road is contested from month one. The Trace's barricade, and later its wall, exist because of the Harker brothers.
- **The Harbour** treats crews as pests until the Mas Camp hits a fish convoy, and then Judge hunts them, and the crews learn to fear the dock boys more than each other. Some join him. Rust never does.
- **The Hills** get raided for food and respond by rooting the raiders who die there, which is the first time anyone sees a rooted kid, and the Gardener comes from somewhere near that.
- **The Gardener** recruits from the crews. Kids with nothing and nobody are the easiest to offer a family in the ground.
- **The crews are the island's weather.** Smoke on the coast road is a crew. A price going up at the Harbour is a crew. A road house burned is a crew. The protagonists rarely see the cause; they see the ripple ([08](08-factions-and-politics.md#how-small-things-move-the-world)).

## Escalation over three years

| Phase | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Month 1 | Nobody fights. Everybody's looking after sleepers. Theft is from empty houses. |
| Month 2 to 3 | Crews form. The Junction tries the barricade. Tock's handprint. |
| Month 4 to 6 | The Mas Camp. Raids on the road and the Hills. The Trace builds its wall. Judge starts hunting crews. |
| Month 6 to 12 | The Harbour siege. Guns used in anger for the first time. The island learns the Trace's twins can't be beaten and that the Trace can be starved. |
| Year two | Crews with cars become crews on foot. The Mas Camp becomes something like a faction. Judge and the Gardener. The first rooted fighters. |
| Year three | The second bloom. A hundred new powered kids and nobody to teach them. Open. |

## The rule for chapters

Powers on the page do work before they do damage. A chapter where Tock only burns someone is a chapter where we forgot he welded the gate that morning.
