# 21. Style sheet

Every rule on this page is read off chapters 1 to 8 (`LATEST.md`, 2 November 2026), with counts where counting helped. Nothing here is invented: where the chapters agree, that agreement is the rule; where they disagree, the case is listed under [Inconsistencies to fix](#inconsistencies-to-fix) with a recommended form for Jaedan to accept or strike. Numbers after a chapter link are line numbers in that chapter's `LATEST.md`. The writing rules themselves live in [14](14-writing-rules.md); this page is the copy-editor's half of them.

### Vocabulary that marks the spelling

The chapters use British and island words for everyday things: *torch* ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 9), *petrol* for what goes in a car ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) 161, [ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 13), *windscreen* ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 101, [ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) 117), *car park* ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 97), *sleepsuit* ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 117), *fridge*, *tap*, *flat* (Kit's, [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 21), *front room*, *exercise book* ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 87), *Form One*. They never use *flashlight, gasoline, faucet, apartment, diaper, gotten* or *Mom*.

One island usage looks American and is correct: **gas** is cooking gas (*the gas truck* [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 87, the *cylinder* [ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) 21) and the **gas station** ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 153, [ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 19). The rule is *petrol* in the car, *gas* in the kitchen and on the station sign.

## Numbers

**Spell out** every number in narration and dialogue: counts, ages, temperatures, breaths, pulses, fuel, distances, people. Compound numbers take a hyphen (thirty-four, seventy-four, fifty-eight). Hundreds take British *and*: "two hundred and twelve" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 43), "three hundred and ten" ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 135), "fifteen thousand" ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 113). Readings are words with no symbol: the oximeter "stayed at ninety-four" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 11), "Pulse fifty-two" ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 15), "breathing eleven" ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 57).

**Digits** appear in four places only, and nowhere else:

| Case | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The emergency number | 999 ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 123, [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 23, [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 67) |
| A year | 1998 ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 13) |
| Text reproduced as written or displayed, in italics | Tock's text ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 159); *Day 0* ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 171); *Mr. Hale. 5:12 a.m. Day 5.* ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 123); *24 KIDS. SEND HELP* ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 151) |
| One narrated clock time | "At 12:14 the lights went out." ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) 149) |

**Times** are words: "around ten o'clock" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 23), "half past nine" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 87), "a quarter to five" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 109), "ten to five" ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 5), "ten to six" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 67), "two in the morning" ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 59), "twelve minutes past five" ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 119), "at nine" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 3). *a.m.* (lower case, stops) appears only inside Juno's notebook.

**Days.** Narration counts with ordinal words: *the first night* (eight uses), *the second day* ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 87), *the fourth day*, *the fourth evening* ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 65, 91), *the fifth night* ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 9), *the sixth morning* ([ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) 7), *the seventh morning* ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 67), *the eighth day* ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 3). Juno's notebook counts *Day 0*, *Day 5*, *Day 7*: capital D, digit, starting from zero on the Sunday because "she wanted to count from the beginning" ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 171). When narration names the notebook heading it drops the italics: "in the book under Day 7" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 151). The bible's *day zero*, *night one* and *week one* are planning shorthand and never appear in a chapter.

**Ages** are words in the chapters ("seventy-four", "ninety", "a nine-year-old", "eight months old") and digits in the bible ("Ida Moss, 16"). Keep both habits.

**House numbers** are spelled and lower case: "Mrs. Moss at number four" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 87), "number nine" ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 127). The bible's *No. 4* is table shorthand.

**Channels**: "Sixteen, the one every boat kept open" ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) 71), "channel sixteen" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 85).

**Family possessives.** Plural families take *-s'* or *-es'*: the Vales', the Mosses', the Finches', the Hales', the Pells', the Quills', the Larks'. One person takes *'s*: Dr. Moss's, Mr. Hale's, Mr. Finch's.

**The "the X girl" pattern.** Unnamed kids are named by family or job: the Nettle girl, the Vale girl, the doctor girl, the Pell baby. It is how the island talks about children it hasn't been introduced to.

## Punctuation

### Speech

- Straight double quotes for speech and straight apostrophes. No curly quotes anywhere in eight chapters.
- No nested single quotes. A phrase quoted inside speech goes in italics: "Not *I'll ask her*. Ask her." ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 179); "know what *will be stopped* mean" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 163).
- **"Said" only.** 180 uses. Subject before verb every time ("Juno said" 55 times, "he/she said" 38), never "said Juno". Beats replace tags whenever they can ("Juno stood up.", [ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 97). The only exceptions are listed under Inconsistencies.
- A flat question ends in a full stop when the voice doesn't rise: "Juno. What is this." ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 53), "Why." ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 23), "Can we." ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 161), "Who thinks a boy should have a crooked arm..." ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 13). Use it for flatness, not as decoration.
- **Exclamation marks** are for a shout across distance and nothing else. Six marks in four lines across eight chapters: "Mr. Wick! Good morning!" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 67), "Mind the police!" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 81), "Juno! There's somebody at the bend." ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 235), "*Ship!* There's a *ship*!" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 103).

### Dashes and ellipses

- **No ellipses.** None in eight chapters. Speech that trails off ends with a full stop.
- **Em dash, unspaced, only inside speech**: cut off at the end ("Two—" [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 89; "I rolled them, I—" [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 99) or picked up mid-flow at the start, as a radio fades in ("—anybody." [ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) 77; "—*sécurité*" [ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 89). Fourteen in the text, all in dialogue.
- **Never in narration.** Narration uses commas, full stops and the occasional colon. No en dashes anywhere.
- The only other em dash is the spaced one in each chapter heading (see [Formatting](#formatting)).

### Other marks

- Semicolons are rare: seven in narration across the book, one in Judge's dialogue ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 187). Treat more than two in a chapter as a smell.
- Colons introduce a list or a rule: "The book said: turn the head to the side." ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 11).
- Hyphens: half-built, burglar-proofing, nine-year-old, red-faced, fridge-cold, tap-cool.
- Abbreviations in capitals, no stops: AIS ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 79), IV ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 143), TV.
- No bold anywhere in the chapters.

## Dialect spelling

The principle is [14](14-writing-rules.md): light island English, readable by anyone, never phonetic. Grammar and word choice carry the dialect. Spelling stays standard. And per 14, **dialect is class**: the Harbour carries more than the Trace; Juno carries least, because she has been correcting herself since Form One; a Trace kid dropping into heavier dialect means something is wrong with them.

| Standard, finished sentences | Ida, Granny (remembered), Nell, the navy | Ida "talked the way Granny talked, every sentence finished properly, like she was reading it off a card" ([ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) 127). Granny's register is church English, not dialect |

No Trace kid has yet dropped into heavier dialect under stress. When one does, per 14, it is a signal.

### Never

Phonetic respelling of any kind: *dem, dat, tink, wid, gyal, doh, wha*. Apostrophes for dropped letters: *goin'*, *'cause*. Alternative spellings of *allyuh* (*all-yuh, allyou, all you*). An apostrophe on dialect *Is*. Italics on dialect.

## Formatting

- One chapter per folder: `chapters/chNN-slug/LATEST.md`, slug in lower case (`ch06-sixteen`).
- Line 1 is the only heading, exactly in this form, with a spaced em dash, an unpadded number and a one-word title: `# Chapter 6 — Sixteen`. All eight chapters match. That em dash is the only one allowed outside dialogue.
- Line 2 is blank. Line 3 is the first paragraph, and it names the POV character (14).
- One paragraph per line, a single blank line between paragraphs, no indents, no trailing spaces, no double blank lines. All eight files pass.
- **Scene breaks** are `---` alone on a line, with a blank line either side. Two breaks (three scenes) in seven chapters; three breaks in ch 7 (see Inconsistencies). No break before the end of the chapter: the last line is prose.
- **Notes to self** go in an HTML comment on its own line, never inside a paragraph: `<!-- POV: Sol. Three scenes: set up, complicate, turn. -->`. `scripts/new-chapter.sh` seeds one after the heading. None of the eight `LATEST.md` files has one now. Strip them before `save-version.sh`, because `build-manuscript.sh` copies `LATEST.md` verbatim into `manuscript/full.md`, and a comment hidden in a preview is still in the file anyone exports.
- Italics with single asterisks. No bold, no underscores, no other Markdown in chapter text.
- Bible pages cite chapter lines as `[ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) l. 61` or, in tables, chapter link and line number.

## Inconsistencies to fix

Recommended forms first. Each is small; none needs a rewrite.

1. **Adverbs on "said".** "'Vee,' Tock said, very quietly" ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 61) and "'Oh,' Juno said, very quietly." ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 201). **Recommend:** cut *very quietly* both times. At 61 the next clause ("and that was that") already does the work; at 201 "Oh" is quiet on its own. Borderline and fine to keep: "He said it plainly, the way Fen would read out a fuel gauge" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 43) and "He said it simply." ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 175), which describe delivery in a separate sentence rather than tag a line.
2. **The one non-"said" tag.** "her father called 'Mind the police!' after it the way he always did" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 81). **Recommend:** keep. It is a habit narrated, not a tag on a conversation. Whitelist it in the linter.
3. **Verandah or porch at the Mosses'.** *Verandah* at [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 87 and [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 5 (Mrs. Moss's verandah chair); *porch* at [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 43, [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 127, [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 141 for the same house. **Recommend:** *verandah* for the Mosses' everywhere. Keep *gallery* for the flat at the bend ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) 99, [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 7, [ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 193) and *balcony* for the upstairs at the Vales' and Hales' ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) 153, [ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 151). Three words, three houses, never swapped.
4. **"The six" means two things.** In ch 5 "the six" is the six sleepers who got IV bags ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 57 "under the six names", [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 87 "Now the six were on syringes", [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 185 "All six of them had"). Everywhere else, and in the whole bible, "the six" is the friends ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) 195). **Recommend:** at [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 87 write "the first six", the phrase ch 8 already uses for those sleepers ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 201). [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 57 and [ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) 185 are clear in context.
5. **"Dr. Moss's front room"** ([ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) 109) against "the Mosses' front room" five times. **Recommend:** "the Mosses' front room".
6. **Judge's two standard verbs.** "Ears hears everything." ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 147) and "a girl who makes ice" ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 175, twice) break his pattern, in the same chapter as "Nell say" and in the same sentence as "it come out the sea". **Recommend:** "Ears hear everything" and "a girl who make ice". If "Ears hears everything" is a deliberate echo of Juno's "Fen hears everything" ([ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) 27), the echo survives the change.
7. **Tock's dialect.** [14](14-writing-rules.md#dialect-is-class) says Ambrose and Tock carry the most dialect on the hill. 9. **Miles for the dust.** "four thousand miles of it" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 9) is the only land or air distance not in kilometres. **Recommend:** "six thousand kilometres of it", low priority. Feet for heights and depths stay.

## Checks Studio can automate

A linter over `chapters/*/LATEST.md` can enforce all of these. Fail means block a version save; warn means show it.

1. **Heading.** Line 1 matches `^# Chapter [1-9][0-9]* — [A-Z][a-z]+$`, and it is the only line starting with `#`. Fail.
2. **Scene breaks.** Count of `^---$` lines is 2. Warn otherwise.
3. **Quotes and dashes.** No curly quotes, no `...` or `…`, no en dash. An em dash only in line 1 or touching a `"`. Fail.
4. **Tags.** After a closing quote: flag `asked|replied|whispered|shouted|muttered|yelled|snapped|murmured|answered|called` (whitelist [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) 81). Flag `said,? (very |so )?\w+ly` and `\w+ly said`. Warn.
5. **American spellings.** color, gray, -ize/-izing (whitelist *galvanize*), kilometer, liter, meter, neighbor, favorite, center, gotten, flashlight, gasoline, faucet, apartment, diaper, Mom, `\bOK\b`, and *towards* (house form *toward*). Warn.
6. **Phonetic dialect.** `\b(doh|dem|dat|tink|wid|gyal|gonna|goin'|'cause|all-yuh|allyou)\b`, and `Is'`. Fail.
7. **Digits.** Any `[0-9]` outside line 1, outside `*...*`, and not in the whitelist (999, four-digit years, [ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) 149). Warn.
8. **Bible words leaking.** `the Sleep`, `day zero`, `night one`, `the Harbour`, `the Hills` in chapter text. Warn until a chapter coins them, then retire the check.
9. **Names.** Any old cast name from `scripts/rename-cast.py` (Imani, Danny, Winston, Jaylen and the rest). Fail. Also warn on `Old Mr\. Hale` mid-sentence, `Moss's front room`, and `porch` within a paragraph that mentions the Mosses.
10. **Boat names.** `Lady Marisol` not wrapped in asterisks. Fail.
11. **Island words in italics.** `\*(doubles|corbeaux?|allyuh|galvanize|gallery|headtie|pirogues?)\*`. Warn.
12. **Exclamation marks.** More than two in a chapter. Warn.
13. **Tics from 14.** "Nobody moved." / "Nobody said anything." more than once; "the way" more than once a scene; *glasses* more than once; filter words (saw, heard, felt, noticed, realised, thought) counted per thousand words. Warn with counts.
14. **Length.** Words outside 2,550 to 3,450 (about 3,000, per 14). Today that flags ch 1 (2,310), ch 2 (2,370), ch 3 (2,045), ch 6 (2,549) and ch 8 (2,536). Warn.
15. **Hygiene.** HTML comments present (warn before `save-version.sh`), trailing spaces, double blank lines, missing final newline. Warn.
