# 22. Life before the Sleep

What the island and its people were like the week before day zero. Anything cited (chapter and line, or a bible page) is canon. Anything tagged **(new)** is invented for this page and is the author's to keep, change or strike; none of it is on the page yet. Where a chapter or the bible leaves a question open, this page leaves it open too and says so.

The author's brief for this page: everyone does a lot of their own things, and it really ranges. These are real people. Use them that way.

## The island on an ordinary Saturday

The Saturday before day zero (new framing; everything in this paragraph is new unless cited). The boats went out at four and were back by eight, and the Finches' plant sold ice to them at the dock in the dark. Lonny was loading timber at the lumber yard on the coast road by seven. Tock was grinding burglar-proofing at a fabrication shop below the junction. Sol was in the plant office doing her mother's Saturday sums. Lyra was at the dance company's Christmas rehearsal in town. Juno was tutoring two Form Fours in Chemistry at the Mosses' dining table, which was still a dining table. Kit was in the yard at the bend with a bucket, because Ambrose had a wedding run at four. Fen was on his roof. Granny Dot was at the Saturday market by six with Juno's shopping list in her head and nobody's opinion but her own. It was hot and nothing was wrong.

### Work and money

The island ran on fish, the port, the hotels and government jobs, in that order of how many people it fed. Fishermen went out before light and sold at the market by mid-morning; the ice plant is what made a catch last past lunch ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L127; Judge, [ch 8](../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) L135). Town had the clinic, the hospital, the police station, the bank, the garage on the coast road with its two apprentices ([13](13-resources-and-roles.md#cars-and-machines)), the gas station, the warehouses, the mas camp, and the lumber and hardware yard (new). The Shore hotels employed housekeepers, cooks, bartenders and the boys who rented kayaks to tourists (new). The Hills grew cassava, plantain, dasheen and breadfruit and sold it in town on Saturdays ([13](13-resources-and-roles.md#food)).

Money was tight and talked about out loud. Nobody on the Trace is rich ([06](06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house)). Most people had a second thing: a sou-sou hand (a rotating savings club where ten people each put in every month and one person takes the whole pot), a pepper sauce business, a cake order book, a PH car on weekends (all new as specifics). Teenagers hustled too: nails, braids, phone screens, tutoring. Prepaid phone credit was its own small economy, bought in $20 top-ups at the parlour. The parlour at the junction kept a credit book for regulars (new). Rough prices for the prose (new; the currency is just "dollars"): doubles $6, a maxi to town $5, a box lunch $35, a set of nails $150.

### School

Kids moved through the same system the whole region uses (new as specifics; the author can rename it):

| Stage | Ages | What it meant in November |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary: Infants, then Standard One to Five | 5 to 11 | Spelling tests on Fridays. The Christmas concert in December. Standard Five sits the **SEA** in May, which decides your secondary school, and the whole year is extra lessons. |
| Secondary: Form One to Five | 11 to 16 | Form Five sits **CSEC** in May and June. November is coursework (SBA) panic and Christmas-term tests. |
| Sixth Form: Lower and Upper Six | 16 to 18 | **CAPE**, two units a year. Scholarships are decided here. |

Where everyone was on day zero (new, derived from canon ages): Juno, 17, Lower Six. Tock, Fen, Lyra, Sol and Ida, 16, Form Five, CSEC in seven months; Tock and Ida have been in the same year since Form One ([ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) L127). Lonny, 17, re-sitting CSEC Maths while working Saturdays. Kit, 15, Form Four. Bix, 11, Standard Five, SEA in May. Olli, 7, Standard One. The Nettle girl, 12, Form One; her brother, 9, Standard Three. Pip, 5, Infants.

Town had two secondary schools (new): the old denominational College up by the churches, which you got into on SEA marks, and the government secondary on the coast road. Most of the Trace kids passed for the College. Lonny and Cass went to the government secondary. Kit still goes to her town school and gets dropped on Ambrose's first fare. Which school you went to was the first thing anyone asked, and it answered every other question.

**Juno's scholarship.** Canon: top of the island "last year", the picture in the paper, the letter in August, "Now you have to keep it" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L57, [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L19). Reading for this page (new): she sat CSEC at the end of Form Five, came first on the island, and in August the letter offered her the island's open scholarship to study medicine abroad, conditional on her CAPE results in two years. "Keep it" is literal. See Conflicts.

### Church and Sunday

Sunday had a shape everyone on the island knew. Early service, or the late one. Church clothes. The service on the radio for anyone who stayed home, which is why Ambrose's car radio had a pastor in it on day zero, losing his place ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L15, L87). Sunday lunch at one. An afternoon drive, the beach, or the cricket on TV ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L35). Granny's white headtie ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L19).

The two steeples in town ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L17) are the Anglican church and the Catholic church (new). There is a Pentecostal tabernacle in what used to be the cinema, a mandir and a small mosque on the back streets, and in the Hills a chapel in every village, Mother Hesper's among them ([supporting](cast/supporting.md#the-hills)) (all new except Hesper). The church in the Hills does things the town churches don't approve of (new; useful when the rooting starts there).

The six's Sunday deal with Granny (new): the Vale children went to the seven o'clock service with her, and then they were free for the beach. That is why Granny was home in her white headtie by mid-morning and the six were in the pickup by ten on day zero. Lyra and Fen's family belong to a church group ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) L17) that has brought food to the Quill house every Sunday for a year (new).

### Music and sound

Soca on every radio ([06](06-island.md)), including some old Machel tune that one station played on a loop all of night one ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L63). By November the new soca for next Carnival was starting to drop and every maxi had it on (new). Parang season had just begun: Spanish-language Christmas music, cuatro and maracas, sides going house to house in the Hills (new). Chutney soca at Indo-Caribbean weddings. Dancehall from the speakers of boys on the sea wall. Gospel on Sunday morning. Fen's Western indie in Fen's ears only ([Fen](cast/fen.md)).

The island's ordinary sounds, for contrast with after: the gas truck's jingle on Tuesdays and Saturdays (new; the truck came late on day zero, [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L87); dogs barking at it ([06](06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house)); the ice plant compressor; church bells; cricket commentary off verandahs; roosters at three in the morning; the bars by the harbour; maxis with the bass up. After day zero most of this goes at once, and the dogs bark at the quiet ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L155).

### Sport

**Cricket** is the adults' religion and the road's game. The Finches were in front of a Test on TV when they fell ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L35). The road played windball cricket (taped tennis ball) in the street, and old Mr. Hale kept the good red ball for a year when it went in his yard ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L169-175).

**Football** is the kids'. Everybody had a Premier League team and the arguments were loud. Sol is Arsenal ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L37). Lonny and Tock were United and Liverpool and hated each other for ninety minutes a week (new). The road's older boys played small-goal football against the Trace boys on the court by the junction on Saturday evenings (new), with Cass as captain of the road side ([supporting](cast/supporting.md#quarry-trace)).

Also (new): netball for girls at both schools; the athletics meet in March; dominoes and draughts under the market shed, mostly old men and one fisherman's son; an open-water swim round the point every Easter.

### Phones and internet

Prepaid phones, rationed data, Wi-Fi passwords traded like currency (new as specifics). What people actually used (new): WhatsApp for everything, including voice notes, prayer chains and the Trace's road group (*Quarry Trace Neighbourhood*, Mrs. Moss as admin, mostly messages about the gas truck, stray dogs and who had a key); TikTok and YouTube for the kids; Instagram for anyone selling something; Facebook for the parents and the church. The six had their own chat, named with a single goat emoji, which Olli is not in (new; the goat stays unexplained, [14](14-writing-rules.md#the-groups-private-language)). Games: Free Fire and EA FC on phones, older consoles in a couple of bedrooms. Netflix on one shared password per road.

On day zero phones still work ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L123, [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L159). Cell service is gone by day 2 to 3 ([02](02-the-sleep.md#infrastructure-in-order-of-failure)); Sol's phone said *No Service* from 3 a.m. on day one ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L37). Every group chat on the island stops mid-conversation.

### Getting around

One coast road, and roads up the valleys to the Hills. Maxi taxis (minibuses) ran the routes, colour-banded by route, sound systems up (new as specifics). Licensed H-cars and unlicensed PH ("private hire") cars filled the gaps; Ambrose drove PH for years before he got his H licence ([06](06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house)). Pickups with kids in the tray were normal and illegal in roughly equal measure ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L81, "Mind the police!"). Bicycles were for small boys and old men (new), which matters when the petrol goes bad ([07](07-powers-in-practice.md)).

### The ferry

The ferry to the bigger island ran twice a day from the dock at Morne Bay (new: the destination is open; canon only that the dock exists and the ferry sat lit in it on night one, [ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L153). It was how you reached anything the island didn't have: specialists, the big hospital, the mall, a visa office (new). Mrs. Quill's oncology appointments were across the water, which is why Lyra has the oncologist's number "from the thing in July" ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) L17; the ferry link is new). Christmas barrels from relatives abroad came in by cargo boat in November and sat in the port warehouse until someone paid the duty (new; see Juno).

### Power and water

Power went in storms and came back in hours; the Quills' generator is for "hurricane days" ([ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) L161). Water came from the mains on a schedule, which is why every house has black tanks on stands ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L17). In the dry season the hill only got mains water at night, and the road's WhatsApp group lit up when it came (new). In November the tanks are full ([06](06-island.md#seasons)). People were used to running out of something and coping. Nobody was used to everything at once.

### Crime and fear

Burglar-proofing on every window ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L17). Ambrose bought his shotgun after a robbery on a night fare ([06](06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house)). Spare keys under the frog on the step, because on the Trace you trusted the road ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L139). Below the bend you didn't, quite. New as texture: girls didn't walk the coast road after dark; the junction parlour got held up the year before; police roadblocks on Friday nights; the bigger island's murders on the evening news, the island's own few known by name. Fear was ordinary and specific: a road, an hour, a car you didn't know. After day zero it becomes general, and nobody is used to that.

### What the tourists saw, and what the island lived

Tourists saw the Shore: white hotels, a lighthouse, reef dives on the wrecks, a fish fry on Friday nights, a Carnival they had seen on a poster (new). They didn't see the water schedule, the clinic queue at Dr. Moss's "Walk-Ins Welcome" ([supporting](cast/supporting.md#quarry-trace)), the ferry for every specialist, or the tally of fares in a taxi man's book ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L5). The high season starts in December; on day zero the hotels were half full and filling (new). What happens to the guests asleep in those rooms is the Shore's problem, and nobody else's.

## What everyone was into

### Juno Vale, 17

Canon base: brilliant, silly, the nursing textbook from the library, the moustache on the newspaper photo, the beach, the bored voice, depression since the attempt two years ago ([Juno](cast/juno.md)).

- **She does Granny.** A perfect impression: the voice, the finished sentences, "So. That's done." The six used to beg for it, and she did it at the beach on day zero (new). She will not be able to do it again for a long time, and the first time she does is a measure of healing ([14](14-writing-rules.md#the-groups-private-language)).
- **Tutoring money in a Danish butter cookie tin.** Chemistry and Maths for Form Fours, $100 an hour at the Mosses' table, saved toward Canada and the cost of being a scholarship student abroad. On the island a blue cookie tin always has sewing things in it; hers is the only one that has money (new). After the Sleep the money is paper.
- **She cannot ride a bicycle.** Granny thought they were dangerous and for boys. At 17, with the petrol going bad, she has to learn on the quarry floor in front of the little ones (new; a set piece for year two).
- **K-dramas, secretly.** Korean medical dramas with subtitles, on her phone under the sheet, with her own running correction of their procedures. Granny called them "the Chinese cartoons" (new). She keeps a ranked list of fictional doctors in the back of an exercise book.
- **Library prefect.** The library she hid in two years ago, where she found the 1998 nursing book ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L13), is where she now has a badge and a key (new). She has never told anyone why she asked for that post.
- **Saturday market sums.** She walks the market with Granny and does the change in her head before the vendor finishes, which Granny never once praised and always relied on (new).
- **The Christmas barrel.** Her father in Toronto ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L27) sends a barrel every year: tins, clothes, a jar of peanut butter the size of Olli. This year's was shipped in October and is sitting in the port warehouse with VALE on the side (new). It is in Judge's town. It may become a plot object: food, clothes, maybe a laptop, and a father's handwriting on the lid.
- **Her room** (new): next to Granny's, sharing a wall she hears the prayer through ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L125). CAPE timetable in four highlighter colours over the desk. The nursing book is not in it; it is back in the library.

### Tock Vale, 16

Canon base: hot-headed, the fete nick at fourteen, the bathroom door at twelve, road cricket, mostly jokes about Lonny, calm only when making something ([Tock](cast/tock.md)).

- **Saturdays at a fabrication shop** below the junction, grinding and painting burglar-proofing for $150 a day (new). He was never allowed to strike an arc. He has watched a thousand welds and knows exactly what a good bead looks like and that he's never made one. When the heat comes, he already knows what it's for. Supports the approved pride in making things.
- **Pepper plants in buckets** along the Vales' back wall: scotch bonnet, a scorpion pepper he got from a boy at school, a seasoning pepper for Granny. He makes pepper sauce in rum bottles and sells it at school for $25 (new). His hands were always burning before his hands were burning. The seeds go into the quarry garden.
- **Junior choir until his voice broke.** He sang in Granny's church choir from seven to thirteen and quit the week his voice dropped (new). He still knows every bass line. This is where the humming comes from ([Tock](cast/tock.md), PROPOSED on the page).
- **EA FC with Lonny**, Liverpool against Lonny's United, every Friday night at the Hales' on Lonny's console, loudly (new). The rivalry is the source of most of the Lonny jokes.
- **"You're Juno Vale's brother?"** Every teacher, every year since Form One. He is average at school and good at Technical Drawing, and nobody at the College has ever asked him about the drawing (new).
- **The fete at fourteen.** Canon: a nick through the eyebrow and a dizziness he still measures things against ([ch 7](../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) L177, L189). What happened is open. Option (new): a boy's fete in town he was too young for, a fight on somebody else's behalf, a cooler lid; he came home at two and Granny was sitting up in the dark.
- **His room** (new): a bunk with Olli on the bottom, Tock on top. Olli has copied his walk ([Olli](cast/olli.md)) and also his habit of sleeping with one foot out.

### Olli Vale, 7

Canon base: counts himself, puts his foot in drains, tongue between his teeth, fell off the Hales' tank stand in August ([Olli](cast/olli.md), [ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L41, L169).

- **His prayers are a census.** Granny made him say them every night and he added "God bless" and everyone on the road by name, in house order, including the dogs (new). It is why he counts himself. It is also, later, a list of who isn't there.

- **The Christmas concert.** He was a shepherd, with a line ("We have come to see the baby") he practised on Tock all November (new).

### Fen Quill, 16

Canon base: the roof, the dongle bought with birthday money, AIS and fishing boats at four in the morning, Western indie, his father's boat on Sundays "before", quit dance at twelve, the taxi with Kit ([Fen](cast/fen.md), [ch 3](../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) L65, L81, L141).

- **Phone screens for money.** He replaces cracked screens and batteries for kids at school, $60 a screen, from a tackle box of tiny screwdrivers and suction cups, with his father's soldering iron (new). After the Sleep the tackle box is the Trace's electronics kit.

### Sol Finch, 16

Canon base: Arsenal, the cold room, her mother's clipboard, autistic, gay and out to no one, laughs first, does the sum ([Sol](cast/sol.md)).

- **A match notebook.** Every match she has watched since she was nine: date, score, scorers, one line. About six hundred entries (new). Arsenal v Everton, Monday at four, has a line ready with no score ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L37).

## Using this page

This page is a store cupboard, not a scene. The rule is [14](14-writing-rules.md#characters): a detail appears only when the scene needs it, and backstory comes out under pressure, to another character, or when something in the room triggers it. Never as a list, never in a paragraph that stops the action.

The best use is grief in passing: a pair of ear defenders on a hook, a fantasy league table nobody updates, forty cake orders in a book, a tadpole jar gone green on a windowsill. One object, no explanation, then back to the work. The second-best use is skill: what someone knew how to do before (guppies, Spanish, dominoes, a fare map of the island) arriving exactly when the Trace needs it, and costing them something to use. The third is jokes: the Granny impression, Lonny's dancing, Fen on cricket scoring duty. When one of these comes back after a death, that is the group healing ([14](14-writing-rules.md#the-groups-private-language)).

Don't let every character's hobby turn into a plot device. Most of what's here should never matter. That is what makes it real.

## Conflicts found

1. **Fen's worst year and Kit's arrival.** Fen's edge was "last year, at the worst of it" ([ch 6](../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) L59), and what pulled him back was Kit and the taxi ([Fen](cast/fen.md#history)). But Kit only arrived in March, eight months before day zero ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L51; [09](09-relationships.md#fen-and-kit-the-taxi)). Either the worst stretch ran into this spring, or "last year" means the last school year. This page avoids dating it.
2. **Sol's "last period".** She planned to watch the 4 p.m. kickoff "on her phone under the desk in last period" ([ch 4](../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L37). Island secondary schools usually finish by about 3 p.m. Either her school runs late, she meant afternoon extra lessons, or the kickoff time moves.
3. **Juno's exam and scholarship.** "Top of the whole island last year" ([ch 1](../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L57) and the scholarship letter "in August" ([ch 2](../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L19) don't say which exam. This page reads it as CSEC results in August of this year, with a scholarship conditional on CAPE. If the author means CAPE (where regional scholarships are usually decided), Juno would have to be in her last school year at 17, and "last year" would need to be "this summer".
4. **Cass's age vs the outline.** The proposed ch 11 calls the son of Cass's mother "awake and thirteen" ([17](17-book-one-outline.md#11--lonny--replaces-the-current-ch-11-fen)); supporting gives Cass as 17. Either Cass has a 13-year-old sibling or the outline line is a slip. This page gives Cass no siblings.
5. **Unassigned households.** [06](06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house) lists "a nurse's household" and "a teacher's pension" on the Trace and assigns neither. This page leaves them unassigned. Not a conflict yet, but a nurse asleep on the Trace would change how ch 5's "Every nurse on the island was asleep" ([ch 5](../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L195) reads.
