# 02. The Sleep

One event causes both the missing adults and the powers: a spore bloom from an ancient fungal network under the island (see [05](05-network-and-gardener.md)). Everyone breathes it in. What happens next depends on the brain it lands in.

## The science underneath

**Brain plasticity.** A teenager's brain is still under construction, rewiring and pruning until the mid-20s. An adult brain is mostly finished. The fungus tries to grow roots into the nervous system. A kid's brain is freshly turned soil, so the roots take. An adult's brain is packed clay, so the roots can't get in, and the body shuts down.

That is what the kids will believe for most of book one. The deeper truth is in [05](05-network-and-gardener.md#what-it-wants): the adults are not being rejected. They are being read.

## Outcomes by age

| Who | What happens | Why (surface) | Why (deep) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adults, roughly 20 and up | The Sleep. Deep dormancy, alive, unreachable. Breathing 11 to 12 a minute, pulse around 50, skin cool, pupils fixed small, no response to pain. | The brain can't accept the roots; the body goes dormant to resist. | The network is reading them. Decades of memory go into its library. It keeps them alive because it isn't finished. |
| Borderline, roughly 16 to 19 | A coin flip. Some sleep. Some awaken with unstable powers. Some sicken and slowly fade. | Half-built brain; biology is never clean. | The network can't get a clean read or a clean root. These are the ones it loses. |
| Kids with a deep groove | A power, shaped like what marked them. Onset over days to months, uneven, like puberty. | Roots follow the deepest grooves carved by experience. | The network is making carriers strong enough to survive the trip off the island. |
| Kids without one | Nothing, yet. Can awaken later after something terrible happens to them. | No groove deep enough. | Not every seed is worth the energy. |
| Babies and toddlers | Sleep normally, feed normally, no powers. | Too little lived experience for a groove. | Open question: will the children who were small when the Sleep came be different later? See [16](16-open-questions.md). |

## The signs, on the day

Day zero is a Sunday in November with Sahara dust in the air out of season. Adults nod off through the morning, wake slow, blame the dust. By mid-afternoon they stop waking. The last to go are the ones who were kept moving (Ambrose at the gate lasted until a quarter to five because Kit kept him on his feet). This matters: **activity delays it by hours, not days.** Nobody outran it.

Animals know first. The corbeaux come down off the light poles and sit on the roof of Ambrose's taxi.

## The age clock

The friends are 16 to 17 at the start. Over two to three years they age into the danger zone. Every birthday is a threat: does the Sleep come, and does having a power protect you? [Juno](cast/juno.md), the oldest, is first in line, and she has no power.

Rules we hold to:
- It is not a hard birthday. It is a window, and nobody knows where their edge is.
- Having a power seems to protect you. Nobody knows whether that is true or just what everyone hopes.
- A second bloom (year three) rolls the dice again for everyone borderline.
- Juno does not sleep at the age line. She awakens: Life/decay, at the cost of her own lifespan. Locked in [Juno](cast/juno.md#her-power-locked). No hint of it before the siege.

## The boundary: a quarantine, not a dome

The outside world detects the outbreak within a day. Navy ships ring the island. Drones pass overhead. Supply drops happen for a few weeks and then stop. Any adult who lands falls asleep on the beach, so nobody can rescue them. The world is watching and choosing not to come.

What the kids see: ships that never come closer, a helicopter that circles and leaves, a boat from the Shore that gets too close and does not come back.

What [Fen](cast/fen.md) hears on the radio, over months: the outside world knows more than the kids do, and the quarantine is not cruelty. See [05](05-network-and-gardener.md#what-it-wants).

## What the kids figure out, and when

- **Night one:** it's only adults; it's the whole Trace; it's probably the whole island. 999 and the hospital don't answer.
- **Day one:** it's the whole island. Other groups of kids exist (the Harbour, via Fen's radio).
- **Week one:** sleepers can be kept alive for a while; bedridden ones start dying by week two; the first rooted sleeper lives.
- **Month one to three:** rooted sleepers survive. The Hills know something.
- **Month three onward:** the network, piece by piece, mostly from Moth and Kit. See [05](05-network-and-gardener.md).
- **Never fully:** whether the sleepers dream, and what wakes.

## How many awaken

About one in ten kids aged 10 to 19 by the end of year one, island-wide. Higher over the deep roots (the Hills, and Quarry Trace on the quarry wound), lower in town. Numbers and what they mean for firepower are in [07](07-powers-in-practice.md#the-numbers-locked).
