# 12. The little ones: babies and small kids after the Sleep

The island has a few hundred babies and toddlers and nobody can find them all. How this plays out is a thread of the whole first book, not a problem the Trace solves on night one.

## Night one: denial

Nobody knows how long this lasts. The working belief, said out loud by almost everyone, is that the adults will wake up, or that someone (government, coast guard, the hospital, the ferry) will come by morning. People act on that belief: they keep their parents comfortable and wait. [Imani](cast/imani.md) half believes it too. The difference is she plans for the other case anyway, quietly, in case.

## What realistically happens in Morne Bay

- **Most small kids are not alone.** Older siblings, cousins, neighbours and the big families that are normal here take them in without being asked. A twelve-year-old with a baby sister feeds the baby. That is the island working the way it always has.
- **The ones who fall through** are babies and toddlers with no older sibling in the house: only children, the youngest in a house where the oldest is five, kids in flats where the neighbours don't know them. Nobody has a list of these. Nobody can.
- **Some kids are already doing what the Trace does.** In every neighbourhood of Morne Bay, some teenager is already walking door to door. The Trace is not special, it is one of many, and later it meets the others. [Judge](cast/judge.md) started exactly this way, organising the little ones before he organised the fish.
- **The cost of the ones missed is real.** In the first week, babies die in cribs in houses nobody checked. The kids who find them carry it. This is the story punishing the gap, and it is why the island's first real argument is about whose job the little ones are.

## What the Trace can actually do on night one

Its own one under-five (the Ramkissoon baby, 8 months), then the twelve houses on the road below the bend that they know by name. Not town. Town has to do town. Imani knows this and hates it. She writes *town?* in the notebook and leaves the question mark.

## Who takes the kids with nobody

| When | Who | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week one | Whoever finds them. The Trace ends up with a few. So does every group. | Nobody sleeps. Formula runs out in days; goat's milk and the Hills become the answer. |
| Month one | The Harbour's children's house at the fish market. The first thing Judge is admired for. | A kid in his house is a family that owes him. His first lever. |
| Later | The Shore's "little ones," a band of under-tens in the beach hotels nobody claimed. | Every faction argues about whose responsibility they are. Nobody takes them. |
| Always | The Trace's position is a fault line: take more in and starve, or close the gate on a five-year-old. | The day it closes the gate on one is the day it stops being the good guys. |

## Practical facts the chapters must get right

- Formula: a few weeks of supply across the whole town, then none. Powdered milk, then goat and cow milk from the Hills, then whatever can be mashed. Babies under six months are the hardest to keep alive and some are not kept alive.
- Nappies: towels, sheets, rinsed in tank water, dried on the fence. The smell is part of the Trace now.
- A toddler needs one person's attention, all day. Every small kid taken in is a teenager who can't do anything else.
- Small kids do not get powers. Whether the ones who were babies at the Sleep grow up different is an open question for book three.

## The crews and the little ones

Crews don't take small kids; they leave them. A raided house with a toddler in it is a toddler alone. The Shore's "little ones" grew partly from that. The Mas Camp is the exception: they take kids who are old enough to hold a cutlass, around ten, which is the thing every faction finally agrees is a line.
