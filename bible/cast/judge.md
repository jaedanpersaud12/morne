# Cole "Judge" Mayers, 17

**Antagonist: the Harbour. Element: Water (the sea).**

## Who he is

A fisherman's son. On the night of the Sleep he was on the deck of the *Lady Marisol* at the fish dock with his father asleep behind him, two hundred kids at the fish market, babies, a boy with a broken arm, a girl who swallowed something, and one radio. He pressed the button and said "anybody" until somebody answered. The somebody was [Fen](fen.md).

By the end of the first week he had organised the boats, the warehouses, the gas station and the children's house. Hundreds of kids eat because of him: 212 bowls of fish broth on day 5, 230 kids on day 7, 310 on day 8 ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), [ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md)). He took the police station's twelve guns on the second day, "before somebody else did," and wears one in a holster he doesn't touch and doesn't hide. He believes, sincerely, that strength is the only thing keeping the Harbour from eating itself. He is the book's Jack, and he has a point.

His father is still on the deck under a blue tarp, a man in his fifties on his side with a cushion under his head. Judge turns him and spoons him water himself.

## Inside

**Want:** The Trace's two assets under his roof: [Juno](juno.md) (the doctor) and [Sol](sol.md) (the ice). In ch 8 he asks Juno to stay, offers to have her grandmother brought down, then names the ice. Later in book one he wants the pharmacy, shared "fairly," meaning under his control ([10](../10-conflicts.md)).

**Need:** **PROPOSED:** Someone he doesn't command. The six have each other to argue with in a kitchen; he has fellas who do what he says, and nobody at the Harbour he can tell he doesn't know what he's looking at.

**Lie:** Strength is the only thing keeping the Harbour from eating itself, and if the number of kids fed is right, the thing he did to feed them was right.

**Fear:** That it comes apart in his hands. He is seventeen with hundreds of children, a dead ice plant, five weeks of rice, a standpipe nobody can explain, and his father asleep behind him on the deck, losing weight.

**Wound:** Night one, alone on the deck, saying "anybody" into a radio that belonged to a man sleeping behind him, until a stranger answered. Whatever happened to him before the Sleep is not on the page (see Open questions).

**How he loves:** By doing the job himself. He goes for the splint slats instead of sending someone, kneels by the boy and holds his good hand, turns his own father every day. He gives before he asks: every gift arrives before the price is named. He tells people the number instead of managing them.

**How it shows:** His face doesn't move much. He slows down when he's thinking and gets careful when he's found something useful. When Fen's name comes up, "something crossed his face and was gone." He walks with his hands behind his back and waits for the other person to speak. Fear comes out as anger (night one, the voice on the radio was "angry") and as plain statements of fact he wants someone else to confirm ("He losing weight").

**Humour:** Dry, short, and used to size people up: "You're shorter than the paper." "Fen's a good boy. Talks too much on an open channel, but a good boy." On night one he laughs at the Trace's thirteen, "a short sound with no fun in it."

**What breaks him:** Losing the siege. The win the Trace pays for with a death is what sends him north to [the Gardener](gardener.md). Cookie is the one person whose loss would end him ([supporting](supporting.md#the-harbours-powered-kids)).

**Happy:** **PROPOSED:** Every boat back by dark, every bowl in Nell's book, and a nine o'clock call that runs long. On a good day he talks to Fen about the sea instead of the arm.

**Secret:** He is lonely, and he envies the Trace six for having each other. He never says it. The only people he admits not knowing things to are outsiders: Fen on the radio ("I don't know what I'm looking at"), Juno on the dock.

**Book one leaves him:** Beaten at the Trace wall, at the fish market after (ch 27 interlude), with Nell counting, Nell questioning him for the first time, and Big Hob fading. He walks north to meet the Gardener. The strongman and the saint. By movement 5 the Harbour's sleepers are going into rows at the fish market ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md)).

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** Pride and command. The fish-box speech, the call sign, "You're what we got."

**Hides:** Terror. Seventeen, hundreds of kids, his father asleep behind him on deck.

**The surprise:** Loneliness. He envies the Trace six for having each other. The nine o'clock calls are business, and they are also the only conversation in his day with someone who isn't under him. "Then I'll come up. Sometimes. To talk."

- "When nobody coming, you go where the work is. Is what I would do. Is what she would do." ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md))
- "My father. Eight days. I turn him. I give him water with a spoon. Nell say that's right." ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md))

Slow when he's thinking. Careful when he's found something useful. "The fellas call me Judge." "Keep this channel on, Fen from the Trace." He carries heavier dialect than anyone on the hill ("allyuh," "Don't vex. Is just how it is," "It have," present tense for the past: "We lose three," "Cookie feed two hundred and twelve"), and that is class, not colour ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#dialect-is-class)). He uses full call signs ("Fen from the Trace," "Trace. You there?"), says numbers flat without hoping, calls his boys "fellas" and the little ones "little fellas," and closes with "Good man" and "Keep the channel on." He never lies; he leaves things to sit instead.

## Relationships

- **[Juno](juno.md).** He needs what she knows and says so, the first person in her life who does. He reads her as his equal in arithmetic ("Is what I would do. Is what she would do") and tells her the number when everyone else manages her; he never lies to her ([09](../09-relationships.md#juno-and-judge-enemies-to-something)). In book one it stays negotiation: trade, the first tax and numbers across a table in ch 19, the thing neither will act on, then the siege, which is personal because they understand each other. **PROPOSED:** she is the one person who could stand next to him instead of under him, which is part of why he keeps asking her to stay.
- **[Fen](fen.md).** The voice on the handheld, every night at nine, a relationship conducted entirely by radio for most of a year. Fen is his only line to the hill and the boy who gave him Juno's name. He likes him ("a good boy") and leans on him ("Not *I'll ask her*. Ask her"). He guessed in ch 6 that Fen was keeping the ship from Juno. **PROPOSED (per [19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#secrets-ledger-who-knows-what-by-chapter-8)):** Juno hears the quarantine from Judge, not Fen, by ch 10; that is the bill for Fen's one more day.
- **[Sol](sol.md).** He knows about the ice (through Ears) and knows its worth: three hundred kids eating fish the day it comes out of the sea. He wants her; Juno says "She's not for sale." Ice for the fish hold in ch 15, where Lux is. Sol doesn't know the Harbour knows. The ice plant is dead and in his town; if Sol wants it running ([sol.md](sol.md), PROPOSED), he is the one who takes that seriously.
- **[Tock](tock.md).** They haven't met. Tock hates him on the strength of one word, *nice*, and two burned handprints. When Judge demands the pharmacy (ch 24), Tock welds the gate.
- **[Kit](kit.md).** He knows she holds the key to Ambrose's shotgun and says he isn't coming for it. Her mother may be asleep in his town; in movement 3 Nell mentions a flat on the coast road with Kit's surname on the mailbox.
- **His father.** The man on the deck of the *Lady Marisol*. The water his father worked is where Judge's power comes from. Judge spoons him himself and nods at "They all are" like a thing he wanted to hear from someone else.
- **Nell, 17, no power.** Runs the books, counts the fish (and every bowl), trades with the Trace, rises inside the Harbour. She meets Juno at the square ("Vale. I'm Nell. He's on the boat. Come.") and writes the twenty-two names. The Harbour runs on her by movement 3; after the siege she questions him for the first time. See [supporting](supporting.md#the-harbour).
- **Dax, 15, Lightning.** His enforcer, and the first kid we see corrupted by what the power rewards. Under Judge he stops a heart for the first time (movement 3) and puts lightning on the Trace's wire in the siege. Not on the page yet.
- **Ears, 13, Air.** His intelligence. See below.
- **Cookie, 14, Fire.** Runs the market kitchen; her oil-drum fires are the lights Fen sees from the roof. The most loved person in the Harbour, and the one whose loss would end Judge.
- **Marsh and Big Hob.** He rations Marsh (fresh water from seawater, at the cost of her kidneys) the way he rations fish. Big Hob, 18 and fading, is the Harbour's walking clock.
- **The Harker boys.** Hold the junction gas station. "I let them keep it. For now."
- **[The Gardener](gardener.md).** The deal after the siege. Judge is not the final villain; he is the person the final villain uses.

## Power

The sea itself. He reads currents, calms water around his boats, and later can drown a man standing on dry sand. It comes from the one thing he always had: the water his father worked. He controls the island's main food source and the only machines that could reach the ships. Not shown on the page by ch 8.

## The grey edge

Every cruel thing he does, he can justify with a number of kids fed. He is right about that number. The reader should half-agree with him until the chapter where the number is wrong.

## Arc

1. **Year one:** the island's only government. Admired. Wants Juno (the doctor) and Sol (the ice). Trades with the Trace, then taxes it.
2. **Year two:** cuts a deal with [the Gardener](gardener.md). Roots the Harbour's sleepers in what look like mass graves to free water and labour, and gains the island's holy man blessing his regime. The strongman and the saint.
3. **Year three:** open. He is not the final villain. He is the person the final villain uses.

## Ears and the gift (added 2026-11-02)

Judge's intelligence is Ears. Everything he knows about the Trace before ch 8 (the generator, the grave, the ice, the gun) came through a thirteen-year-old's hearing. He never hides it ("Don't vex. Is just how it is.") and he never explains it, which is why Juno files it as a nickname. The reader should not. In ch 8 Juno assumes the ice leaked because Fen talked on an open channel; it was Ears.

The twelve bags are the first gift. Rule: every Judge gift is correct, useful, and arrives before the price is named. The second gift comes with the price attached. The third is refused, and that refusal is the first thing the Trace does that Judge takes personally.

## Open questions

- **Where does the envy show first?** Option A: a Judge interlude (ch 27) where he watches the Trace's lit window from the dock. Option B: on the radio, when he hears the six laughing at something he can't follow (the goat) behind Fen.
- **His life before the Sleep.** Is there a mother? Options: dead, so the deck was always the two of them; or alive and asleep in town, a bed he doesn't visit.
- **Why "Judge"?** Did the fellas name him for settling disputes on the dock before the Sleep, or after the first week, for deciding who eats?
- **His father in book one.** Does Judge root his own father in the rows (movement 5), or is his the one bed he keeps on the deck while everyone else's goes in the ground?
- **The age line.** He is seventeen, same as Juno. When is his birthday, and does Big Hob fading make him count it?
- **When does his power first show on the page?** Option: a boat that should have been lost comes back on flat water (Fen sees it through the binoculars); or Juno notices the water still around the *Lady Marisol* in ch 19.
- **Which chapter is the number wrong?** Marsh going grey, or the rows at the fish market?
- **Timing.** This page and [11](../11-timeline.md) put the Gardener deal and the rows in year two; [17](../17-book-one-outline.md) has the walk north in ch 27 and rows by movement 5. Does book one end with the meeting or the first rows?
