# Tock Vale, 16

**Element: Fire. Depth tier 3.**

## Core fact

Granny called his anger sinful, something to pray out of him. He half believes it, which makes it come out worse.

## Who he is

Tobias Vale, called Tock. Juno's younger brother, Olli's older one. Built like a fire hydrant, buzz cut, a white nick through his left eyebrow from a fete at fourteen. Hot-headed, loose, fights anyone who disrespects his people, and the first to have your back. He comes to everything that scares him angry ([ch 2](../../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md), line 37). When he is scared he hums Granny's hymns under his breath (APPROVED 2026-11-02; replaces the old tell "when he goes still, that is the worst sign"). **PROPOSED** on the page: no chapter shows the humming yet.

He was the one who walked past the Pells' crib on night one, and the one who went back. On night one he also checked every house on the road and found old Mr. Hale face down in a puddle of soup. Since the second day he has boiled every drop of water the ward and the babies use, a job nobody gave him.

## Inside

**Want:** Keep Vee. Ch 7 opens on it: he has hidden a three-day fever because if Juno finds out she will take the baby away from him. Everything he chases in book one (control of the heat, the gate, the wall) is so he can go on holding her.

**Need:** To stop asking for the heat to be taken out and learn it answers to calm and love as well as rage. The first steady boil in ch 7 comes when he thinks about Vee asleep on his chest. He also needs someone in his family to say it isn't a sin, and so far only Ida has.

**Lie:** Granny was right about him. "That temper is a fire, and fire belongs to the devil." He says it to her sleeping body in ch 7: "You were right, though. About me."

**Fear:** Hurting someone he loves, and Vee first. The moment his hands leave a print on the counter, his first words are to keep the baby out of the kitchen. Under that, being sent away from her for being dangerous.

**Wound:** Mother dead, father in Canada, raised by a strict, devout grandmother. At twelve he put his fist through the bathroom door after a fight with Juno, and Granny made him kneel on the kitchen tiles and pray it out of him. He prayed until his knees went numb and nothing left. He learned to put it somewhere else instead.

**How he loves:** He holds on and won't give over. Twenty minutes with the screaming baby on night one, four hours in the armchair without moving, a dead arm and still "No." He takes the job nobody assigned and does it every day. He fixes Granny's crooked headtie before he says anything to her. Love from Tock looks like a refusal to let go.

**How it shows:** Anger first, every time. He kicks the leg of the table, not hard, then harder: taking it out on things that can't feel it. When the heat comes he holds his hands up and out, palms away from his own body, like someone has a gun on him. He runs his hands under the cold tap until the water comes out warm. Scared, he hums Granny's hymns under his breath (**PROPOSED** on the page). He repeats himself when he's frightened: "it's okay, it's okay", "No, no, no", "I walked past."

**Humour:** Mostly about Lonny ("He's eating *at* it"). Dry, short, delivered without opening his eyes. He is the one who says "The goat" and sets the whole group off in ch 1, and the one who tells the cricket-ball story at Mr. Hale's grave. The group's joke works on him even when nothing else does ("It's not even a goat," ch 4).

**What breaks him:** Burning a person. Month two he leaves a handprint on a Junction boy's arm and doesn't speak for two days. The bill for ch 7 arrives there: Juno asked "how hot", not "are you safe", and ch 12 is where that costs him. Second, Vee sick and him unable to fix it ("Fix it, Juno," ch 4); in the dry season the whole Trace sees what he is capable of when she is ill, and some of them are afraid ([09](../09-relationships.md#tock-and-the-baby)).

**Happy:** Loud, loose, the fun one. Olli copies his walk. Bix follows him everywhere. A good day is the baby asleep on him, the jugs full before six, and somebody to wind up. When he gets to make something with his hands he is proud of it and calm in a way he never is otherwise (APPROVED; **PROPOSED** on the page until he welds).

**Secret:** On the morning of the ship he knelt at Granny's mattress and begged God to take the heat out of him before he touched the baby, and the praying is what cracked the tile. Ida saw the crack and his knees. Nobody saw what he asked for, or heard him tell Granny she was right.

**Book one leaves him:** The builder of the walls. He leaves the handprint in ch 12 and goes silent, welds the gate in ch 24 when the Trace becomes a fort, and his gate glows through the siege. If Bix dies there (proposed, [16](../16-open-questions.md)), the boy who attached to him is the one the Trace loses. In ch 29, when Juno can't carry Granny to the garden alone, Tock takes the other end. He helps put the woman who called him sinful into the ground, with Vee still his.

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** anger.
**Hides:** shame (Granny's "sin").
**The surprise:** pride and joy in making things; welding is the one time he's calm (APPROVED). On the page so far only the calm boil in ch 7 points at it. The welding itself is **PROPOSED** until it is written.

- "Come back." ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md))

Short sentences, mostly commands and flat statements, and he repeats the line that hurts ("I walked past," "I walked *past*"). He doesn't explain feelings; he says the fact and stops, or he says nothing, which the others read as worse. In his own POV the narration stays plain and physical: hands, heat, the pot, the tile. Granny's words are the only long sentences in his head.

## Relationships

- [Juno](juno.md): she can talk him down. Nobody else can. He is the only person she hits (ch 1, the arm). He has watched her do sums his whole life, and in ch 7 he waits for her to say either "get away from the baby" or "it isn't a sin." She asks how hot he can get it instead, and he becomes a resource before he is a brother. They never break and fight constantly. When she passes the age line, or doesn't, he is the one in the room, and at the end of book one he takes the other end of Granny.
- [Lonny](lonny.md): his partner on every run. The muscle and the heat. Tock's jokes are mostly at Lonny's expense, and Lonny fixes what Tock can't (the regulator, thirty seconds, without looking). When Lonny leaves for the road in months 3 to 6, Tock does the runs alone and it nearly kills him ([09](../09-relationships.md#lonny-and-the-girl-on-the-road)).
- [Olli](olli.md): Tock is the fun one. Olli copies his walk.
- The Pell baby, Vee (Velora, 8 months): he carried her for twenty minutes and wouldn't give her over. She is his, in a way nobody says out loud. He named her. She keeps him from becoming what his anger could make him; she is also the person he is most afraid of burning. She grabs his eyebrow scar when she wants him.
- Bix Hale, 11: attaches to Tock, not his own brother. By ch 7 he hasn't left Tock's side in five days, runs the water jugs on Olli's old wagon, sings to Vee badly. Tock snaps at him and Bix laughs at the oven gloves until Tock looks at him. If Bix dies in the siege (proposed), Tock loses the boy who chose him.
- Granny Dot: his judge. He fixes her headtie, tells her about the ship, tells her she was right. What she says to him if she wakes is the open wound of his arc. Rooted Granny (ch 29) means he may never hear it.
- Ida Moss ([supporting](supporting.md#quarry-trace)): friction, not romance. He has never liked her; she talks the way Granny talked. In ch 7 she is the first person to tell him the heat is not a sin ("the devil doesn't boil water for sleepers") and sets the rule: burn someone on purpose and then they talk about sin. He hates her and listens to her. She says "Language" when he swears.
- [Judge](judge.md): a voice he has never met, who wants his sister and thinks he can send someone up the hill to ask her nice. That word is what burns Granny's table. In ch 8 he says "No," then nothing at the vote, and at the bend tells Juno to come back.
- [Sol](sol.md): she takes Vee without a word when he can't hold her and gives her back the same way. Later, when Sol keeps going to the Harbour, Tock thinks she is passing information, and when the group learns about Lux he is probably the one who says the wrong thing, as a joke, and means it ([09](../09-relationships.md#sol-and-lux-across-the-wall)).
- [Fen](fen.md): Tock watches him decide what to say about Judge in ch 7 and reads the hesitation. When Fen admits he sat on Judge's request, it is Tock who stands up.

## Power

He cannot throw fire. Heat builds inside him with emotion. Angry, it explodes outward: a hand on a doorframe leaves a scorch. Calm, he can focus it into his hands, hot enough to cut and weld metal. The boy whose anger was called sinful becomes the one who builds the walls that keep everyone safe. See [03](../03-power-system.md).

On the page (ch 7): fear and anger boil a pot in seconds and melt its handles; prayer, which is the same thing as the fear, cracks a floor tile; calm, thinking of Vee, gives a steady rolling boil he can hold for a minute and stop. That one pot drops him into a chair. The ward needs eight a day.

**Cost:** fevers, dehydration, hunger, and real fear of hurting someone he loves. After welding a gate he can't stand for an hour. In ch 7, three days of fever before the first use, then dizziness, a dry mouth, three glasses of water and a tin of sardines after one calm boil. Eats for two ([07](../07-powers-in-practice.md)).

## Fault line

Tock vs. himself. Is his power a sin or a gift? Who does he hurt first, and is it on purpose? Ida's two questions in ch 7 ("Did you burn anybody?" "Did you want to?") are the fault line said out loud.

## In a fight

A hand on skin burns. A hand on a cutlass makes the other kid drop it. The gate glows and nobody touches it. Angry, he burns people he didn't mean to; calm, he's slow. Month two he leaves a handprint on a Junction boy's arm and doesn't speak for two days. Daily, he welds, cuts, boils, sterilises. See [07](../07-powers-in-practice.md). Rule: if a chapter has him burn someone, he welds something that morning ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#combat)).
