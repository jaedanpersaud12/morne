# Sol Finch, 16

**Element: Water (ice). Depth tier 3.**

## Core facts

Her family runs the ice plant for the fishermen; her mother runs it "with a clipboard and a voice that made grown fishermen say yes ma'am" ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 151). She is gay, and out to no one.

## Who she is

Autistic, happy, an only child, at number five ([06](../06-island.md#quarry-trace-house-by-house)). Loves football and watches every match she can; she doesn't play. Arsenal is the team she talks about ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), [ch 7](../../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md)). Round and solid. Holds her phone in both hands when she's scared. Turned the TV off when she found her parents on the couch, and turned it back on because the quiet was worse. She never sleeps when things change.

She was already in the Trace before the six noticed her; she lived two doors from Lonny and the group absorbed her somewhere around age nine. Juno was first: at five, Juno walked across the Finches' yard and asked if Sol wanted to see a frog, and in the eleven years since has never once asked Sol a question before Sol was ready for it ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 251). (The chapter is canon; how "five" with Juno and "nine" with the group fit together is under Open questions.) She is the one who laughs first at the goat.

## Core pillar: being seen

She is gay in a place where coming out can cost you your family and your friends. The fear is not abstract. It is the fear of losing the exact people you love most, which is why she has never told them. Where she is at the start of the book (out to nobody, or to one person) is still [open](../16-open-questions.md).

Her story asks the theme's question most personally: when the old world's rules are gone, does the new one grow back better, or rebuild the same prejudices? Who accepts her, who doesn't, and whether the Trace becomes somewhere she can be herself. A crush on Lux, a girl from the Harbour whose family worked the ice plant, ties her to the other side.

Writing rule: let the reader sit in the risk with her before anyone's answer comes. Acceptance from the group should cost something and mean something, not arrive for free. See [14](../14-writing-rules.md#writing-sols-story).

## Inside

**Want:** The ice plant running again (**PROPOSED**, see [Her want, on the page](#her-want-on-the-page-added-2026-11-02-proposed)). On the page so far, the want is smaller and already visible: to do the next practical thing the way her mother would have, an hour earlier (filling every pot in the fifth house, [ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md)).

**Need:** To be seen whole by the people she loves and find out the loss she has braced for is survivable, or isn't, and live either way. The group's acceptance has to cost them something ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#writing-sols-story)).

**Lie:** That the only safe way to keep the people she loves is to keep part of herself frozen: preserved, but hidden (see [The groove](#the-groove)).

**Fear:** Being seen, and losing the exact people she loves most because of it. In ch 4 the fear is already there in small: when Juno gives her the adding-up look, Sol is afraid of the answer to "what else can you do," and more afraid of the question.

**Wound:** No single wound on the page, and the bible doesn't need one ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#characters): not every trait needs a wound). The fear is specific to where she grew up. Something in Form Two was bad enough that Juno sat on the end of her bed through the whole year without saying a word; what it was is open.

**How she loves:** Practically, and without asking for anything back. A slice of bread left by Juno's notebook for an hour; an hour holding her mother's hand, telling her the plan out loud because her mother liked a plan said out loud; Vee handed back to Tock without a word. She asked for nothing for her own parents, and Juno counted that as a reason her father got a bag ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md), line 43).

**How it shows:** Phone held in both hands; phone put face down "very carefully"; crying without a sound ([ch 2](../../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md)). Both hands over her mouth at the ship ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md)). Knees up on her chair at the table, and down fast when something goes wrong ([ch 7](../../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md)). Her hands go cold before she knows why, and she rubs them under the table.

**Humour:** Laughs first, every time, "like a hiccup." Her own jokes are flat statements of fact that land as jokes: "You can sit. You can also eat." The bible counts her as one of the two characters who make a room funny ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#humour-distribution)).

**What breaks her:** Being suspected by the group and being unable to explain without telling them who she is. In ch 15 she takes the accusation over the truth ([09](../09-relationships.md#sol-and-lux-across-the-wall), [17](../17-book-one-outline.md#movement-3-the-pressure-ch-1220-months-26)). Chaos breaks the power too: the ice is best in her rhythm, worse in chaos.

**Happy:** Contentment. A match on, a plan said out loud, a routine that holds. The cold room at the back of the plant, where the air hurts your teeth and nobody talks. The Monday after the Sleep, her first thought was Arsenal and Everton at four, before her parents, and she was ashamed of that and thought about it anyway.

**Secret:** She is gay and has told no one. She has a door in town she has never knocked on ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 165). The Harbour already knows about the ice through Ears ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md)); she doesn't know that it knows ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#secrets-ledger-who-knows-what-by-chapter-8)).

**Book one leaves her:** Having been doubted and having chosen the accusation over the truth (ch 15). Having fought once, in the siege, freezing the wet road under the dock boys (ch 25). With Lux's answer given in movement 5, but what Lux says is still open ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#still-open-for-book-one)). Whether she is out to the group by ch 30 is open.

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** contentment.
**Hides:** fear of being seen.
**The surprise:** not decided. See Open questions.

She talks to the table or to a baby more easily than to a face, says "I don't know" about herself, and her narration thinks in sums and in what her mother would have done.

## Relationships

- [Juno](juno.md): the one who noticed the ice and said nothing. Sol trusts her for that. It goes back to five years old and a frog, and to Form Two, when Juno sat on the end of Sol's bed for a year without asking. What Sol feels is love for being left alone until she's ready ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 251). In book one Juno is also the one who will have to answer Judge when he bargains for Sol ([09](../09-relationships.md#juno-and-judge-enemies-to-something), [10](../10-conflicts.md#moral-questions-with-no-clean-answer)), which puts the person who never asks in the position of deciding for her.
- [Lonny](lonny.md): neighbours, two doors apart. He treats her like a little sister, which she half likes. In ch 4 he hands her glass after glass of water without a word; she reads his silence at Wren's door because she has a door like that too. Both of them have someone outside the wall. Everyone knows about his; nobody knows about hers.
- Lux ([supporting](supporting.md#the-harbour)): the crush. The Harbour. Where Lux stands on Sol being gay is one of the quiet, high-stakes questions of the book. Lux's family worked the plant Sol's family owned, and Sol's power now does their job ([09](../09-relationships.md#sol-and-lux-across-the-wall)). In year one Sol has reasons to go to the Harbour and one real reason. Lux's answer comes in movement 5 and should not be a clean yes; she has a family in a strongman's town and a faith of her own.
- [Tock](tock.md): she holds his baby when he won't, and hands her back without asking about his hands ([ch 7](../../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md)). In book one he is the one who thinks she's passing information to the Harbour, and probably the one who says the wrong thing when she's out, meaning it as a joke ([09](../09-relationships.md#sol-and-lux-across-the-wall)).
- [Olli](olli.md): per [09](../09-relationships.md#sol-and-lux-across-the-wall), the one who says the right thing first, because he doesn't know it's a thing.
- [Judge](judge.md): has never met her and already wants her. "Allyuh have a girl who makes ice... both of us know what that's worth" ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md)). In book one he rations, taxes and bargains for Sol; Sol's ice is the first thing that pulls the Trace toward his table willingly (see her want, below).
- Her parents: both asleep on the couch at number five, then in the rows at the Mosses'. Her mother is strict, by other people's word; Sol never understood why they meant it as a complaint. Her father, forty and strong, coughed on the spoon and got one of the first six bags ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md)). Where they would stand on her being gay is not on the page.

## The groove

The cold room at the ice plant was her quiet place when the world got too loud. She has spent years keeping part of herself frozen, preserved but hidden. The power is shaped like that. In ch 4 it is literally how the first ice comes: she thinks about the door shutting and the sound going away.

## Power

She makes ice on a tropical island, best when she is in her rhythm, worse in chaos. The first sign was a sweating glass of water on night one that nobody but Juno noticed. On the page it is Olli's glass ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 247), and Sol is the one who names it. The second is the pot in the fifth house, sweating against her chest. Ice preserves food, breaks fevers and keeps medicine cold: one of the most valuable gifts in the Trace, and the reason every faction wants her. See [13](../13-resources-and-roles.md#food).

**Cost:** dehydration; she drinks constantly (four glasses after the first bucket). Her hands burn afterwards, the way skin burns coming in from snow, and her teeth chatter in thirty degrees. And the pull: by year two she feels the network's homesickness for the sea more than anyone. See [05](../05-network-and-gardener.md#what-it-wants).

## In a fight, and on a Tuesday

She won't, for a long time. When she finally does (the siege), wet ground freezes under the dock boys' feet and they fall. Daily is where she matters: the fish hold, the medicine box, fevers broken with a hand on the forehead, ice for trade, the pool kept from going bad. Her hands are always cold now. See [07](../07-powers-in-practice.md).

## Her want, on the page (added 2026-11-02, PROPOSED)

Being seen is the pillar; it is not a want a reader can watch her chase in chapter 4. The chase needs an object.

**PROPOSED: Sol wants the ice plant running.** Her mother's cold room, dark since night one. Fish rotting in a day at the Harbour because the plant is dead ("Ice plant's dead, so what we catch we eat the same day or it rot," Judge, [ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md)). It is the one place on the island where Sol's gift is not a miracle but a job, and it is her mother's job, and Lux's family's. Every time she makes ice in a bucket she is doing by hand what the plant did by machine, and she knows it. By movement 3 (ch 15, "Ice for the fish hold. Lux.") the want and the fault line are the same thing: the plant is in Judge's town.

What this gives the page now: in ch 4 she already thinks about the cold room to make the ice. From ch 9 she can say it: "If I could get the plant going." Nobody takes it seriously. Judge does.

The cost that comes with it: the plant needs power, power needs Fen's fuel maths, and the Harbour has the fuel. Sol's want is the first thing that pulls the Trace toward Judge's table willingly.

## Open questions

- **The surprise emotion.** Not decided. Options: (a) anger at being a resource, the first time someone talks about her ice as a price (Judge, ch 8, or Juno answering him); (b) pride, the ice as her mother's job done by hand, which she likes more than she thinks she should; (c) grief for her parents arriving late, under the contentment, triggered by something routine (a match she can't watch). Whichever it is, per [14](../14-writing-rules.md#writing-sols-story) it must not stand in for or shortcut the being-seen arc.
- **Out to nobody, or one person, at the start?** (Still [open](../16-open-questions.md).) Ch 4 reads as nobody. Option: keep it nobody through book one; or make the one person Juno, who knows and has never asked.
- **The door in town** ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md), line 165). Whose? Option: Lux's, which would place Lux in town rather than the Harbour proper; or a door she has thought about knocking on for some other reason, left unexplained until ch 15.
- **Form Two.** What happened that Juno sat through without a word? Option: the year Sol first knew, or nearly got found out; or something not about her sexuality at all, so not every silence points one way.
- **Did Juno notice the glass on night one?** Ch 4 never says. Option: Juno noticed and said nothing (keeps the trust line in Relationships); or Juno didn't, and the "noticed and said nothing" line moves to the bucket.
- **Her parents on her being gay.** Not on the page. Option: leave it unknowable while they sleep, so she can't find out; or let one remembered remark from her mother carry the risk the reader sits in.
- **When does Sol learn the Harbour knows about the ice?** Option: ch 15, as part of the doubt; or earlier, from Juno, which would make Juno complicit in the secrets ledger.
