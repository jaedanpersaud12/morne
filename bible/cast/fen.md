# Fen Quill, 16

**Second POV. Element: Air (Stillness), linked with Lyra. Depth tier 2.**

## Who he is

The tech one. Quiet, laid-back, in the background, respected without trying. Locs tied back, earphones in, looks at the ground beside your foot when he's looking at you. Into Western indie and rock while the island plays soca (a texture, not a plot point). Keeps the generator alive, rigs the radio, listens to the navy. Doesn't do goodbyes on the phone.

He is not "the tech guy." The tech is what he does with his hands while the rest of him is somewhere else.

## History

Their mother's long illness (cancer, four years of it since the first surgery when the twins were twelve; the last year a hospital bed where the dining table used to be, a night nurse on Sundays). Their mum put both twins in a dance company as little kids. Lyra stayed and competed. Fen quit at twelve, the day she came home from the first surgery, and buried it under screens and headphones. Nobody in the group has seen him dance in years. In [ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) (line 157) he tells his sleeping mother when: he came back from the studio, saw the car in the drive, knew "this was going to be a whole year," and never went back. "That's not your fault. I'm just saying, that's when."

During the worst of it he felt cornered, with no way out, and thought about ending his life. What pulled him back was love: [Kit](kit.md), and late-night talks in her father's parked taxi at the bend where nobody in the Trace was watching. It is why she is half inside the group. On the page so far it shows once, sideways: in [ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) (line 59) he recognises Juno's stillness at the table because he sat that way himself "last year, at the worst of it, at a desk in his room with the door locked and a screen on that he wasn't watching." Per [14](../14-writing-rules.md#writing-suicide-and-depression): before and after only, never method, never a twist, and he still has bad days.

He learned the sound of every machine in his mother's room without meaning to, the way you learn the stairs in your own house in the dark. Concentrator: low hum, a click every four seconds. Mattress pump: higher, every twenty minutes. Monitor: silent unless something is wrong, three times in eight months ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), line 5).

He bought the receive-only dongle with birthday money after his father called it a waste. The plastic chair on the flat roof has been up there two years. The handheld is his father's boat radio, from Sundays on the little boat, "before."

## His stance on their mother

He and Lyra agree completely. She suffered for years, and the Sleep is the first time she has been at rest; her hands are open on the blanket for the first time in months. They are at peace with letting her go, and it still hurts. In a story full of fractures, this bond is the one that does not break. The conflict comes from outside: others judging the choice.

Lyra said it first ("Is it bad that she looks better?") and he answered for both of them: "So no. I don't think it's bad." Saying it "felt like putting something heavy down" ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), lines 39-59).

## Inside

**Want:** To keep the house running and Judge handled by himself: fuel in the cans, oxygen through the night, the channel on at nine, so that nobody has to learn what he said on night one ([09](../09-relationships.md#doubt-who-the-group-stops-trusting-and-why)). On the page it is litres and hours: 18 litres a night, 58 in the cans, three nights, then the next car ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), lines 11-15).

**Need:** To say the true thing to the person it belongs to before they hear it somewhere else. Book one makes him pay for not doing it (the ship) and mends him when he does (the quarantine, told to Juno first, [ch 22 in 17](../17-book-one-outline.md#movement-4-the-turn-ch-2127-months-610)).

**Lie:** That carrying something alone is the kind thing. "One more day" got him through the worst of last year, so he gives Juno one more day of believing in the ship ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), line 195). His power is the same lie in a body: he absorbs force, and alone he fills up and it hurts.

**Fear:** Being the reason someone gets hurt, again. He gives Juno's name to a stranger and then lights the only house on a black island, and his verdict is four words: "He had done it again" ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), line 191).

**Wound:** The year of the hospital bed, and the edge he went to inside it. The day of the first surgery is the day he stopped dancing, and the thing he stopped doing is the thing his power will give back.

**How they love:** By doing, never by saying. He pulls Miss Tilda's blanket up over Miss Tilda, checks his father's breathing on the hall floor, carries the paint tins and holds a corner of the sheet he doesn't believe in ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) line 143, [ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) line 151). With Kit, per [09](../09-relationships.md#fen-and-kit-the-taxi), the reader works it out from acts; they never explain their feelings.

**How it shows:** He goes up. The roof is where he goes when something hurts, and he stays there after the funeral "because up here there was nobody to look at him and the machines made sense" ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), line 9). He looks at the ground beside your foot; he takes one earphone out; he counts breathing; he turns the volume down to nothing rather than lie. When something truly lands he takes one step back and no more ([ch 7](../../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md), line 225).

**Humour:** Dry, deadpan, about machines and about Lyra, and rare ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#humour-distribution): one dry line per Fen chapter, from him). "I don't tell Lyra about the generator until I know it works, because she'll want to try it, and she'll flood it." "Everybody we know is sixteen."

**What breaks them:** Finding out his silence cost the person he kept it for. The first bill is Juno hearing about the ship from Judge instead of him ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#decisions-cost)); the deeper one is Kit, whom the island is taking, and whether he can let her go the way he let his mother go ([09](../09-relationships.md#fen-and-kit-the-taxi)).

**Happy:** On the roof at four in the morning with the dongle and a length of wire, listening to taxis, aircraft and fishing boats that don't know they're being heard ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), line 65). The taxi at the bend with the radio low. Before twelve, the studio.

**Secret:** Several, stacked. The taxi nights with Kit. How close he came last year. That he gave Juno's name on an open channel. What the navy actually said ("will be stopped"). That his mother's sats hold at 94 on room air, which he has noticed and is "trying not to think about" ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), line 11; he keeps this from Lyra too, [19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#secrets-ledger-who-knows-what-by-chapter-8)). The drone that looked down at his roof.

**Book one leaves them:** His mother in the garden, carried there by the twins (ch 18), and his father still in a bed in the hall, the sleeper nobody decides about. Seen: the dance on the road (ch 16) ends the secret of Kit and of himself. Mended with Juno by telling her the quarantine is right before anyone (ch 22). At year's end the Gardener asks for Kit by name and Fen is the one who wants the gate shut, the first time he and Kit want opposite things ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md), [09](../09-relationships.md)).

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** steadiness. "Stay with her. Count." The generator on the third pull with his hands shaking on the cap ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), lines 171-179).

**Hides:** relief that his mother is at rest, and guilt about the relief. This is his emotional core, and it is not Juno's depression: Juno's edge is about her own life; Fen's live wire is being glad of something that looks, from outside, like losing her. The relief is on the page ([ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md), lines 41 and 59; the 94% he is "trying not to think about," [ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), line 11). The guilt is approved but not shown yet: **PROPOSED** until a chapter puts it there.

**The surprise:** delight when his body remembers dancing. Approved; the power hasn't come yet, so **PROPOSED** until ch 16 (the garage, the engine block, the road houses).

Machines, sounds, counts. Short declaratives when he's working. He talks to his mother out loud when nobody's listening. He never explains himself to anyone else. His answers to people are mostly "Yeah," "I know," "I'm here"; he lets silences run rather than ask a question ("Fen let it sit, because the alternative was asking"). His English is light, close to standard, with none of Judge's dialect; in his POV the narration names parts and numbers (cannula, transfer switch, AIS, eighteen litres) and almost never names a feeling.

## Relationships

- [Lyra](lyra.md): his twin and his other half, literally. No privacy when they're linked. The feeling is total trust with a protective edge: she is "the only person in the world he could give [a real answer] to," and he still keeps things from her for her own good (the generator until it works, the 94%, what the ship said when she asked "is it for us?"). They clash, and the weather changes, and it holds; [09](../09-relationships.md#fen-and-lyra-the-unbreakable-one) says do not break it for drama. In book one: the power links them (ch 16), Lyra learns through him that Cass is carrying sleepers out (ch 23) and chooses her twin, and they carry their mother to the garden together (ch 18), where he tells her about the 94% (**PROPOSED** timing, per [16](../16-open-questions.md)).
- [Kit](kit.md): the taxi at night. Neither of them has told anyone. She is part of why he is still alive and he has never said so, including to her. On night one he goes to the roof instead of to her, the first failure ([09](../09-relationships.md#fen-and-kit-the-taxi)), and ch 1 to 8 bear that out: after the goat line, he never once goes down to the bend. In book one he is caught between her and Juno and chooses neither out loud; the dance ends the secret; at year's end he wants the gate shut on the Gardener and she does not.
- [Juno](juno.md): the edge they both know. Not romance. He recognises her stillness at the table because it was his. The feeling is kinship, and in ch 6 it turns to the first lie by omission: he doesn't knock, then hands her a half-truth on the roof. The trust break comes when she learns he gave her name to Judge and waited to tell her ([09](../09-relationships.md#juno-and-fen-the-edge)); the ship compounds it. He never apologises ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#cost-audit-for-ch-1-to-8-what-each-turn-cost-and-whether-the-bill-has-come)). It mends with an act: he tells her the quarantine is right before anyone else (ch 22).
- [Judge](judge.md): the voice on the handheld. A relationship conducted entirely by radio for most of a year. Every night at nine: "Fen from the Trace." Fen half trusts him, because "it sounded like he meant everything, which was the problem" ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md), line 39). Judge reads Fen's silences correctly ("No. I didn't think so. The doctor girl don't know") and leans on him. The group comes to doubt Fen because of these nightly calls, and the truth is he is managing the most dangerous person on the island alone ([09](../09-relationships.md#doubt-who-the-group-stops-trusting-and-why)).
- Their mother: the person he talks to. Love and relief together, the relief he can't say to anyone but Lyra. He keeps her oxygen running by fuel arithmetic, and he controls the morphine.
- Their father: on the hall floor, then in the ward ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md), line 7). The sleeper nobody talks about; the twins never decide anything about him in book one ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#side-arcs-under-the-main-plot)). The generator, the binoculars and the radio are all his.
- Ears ([supporting](supporting.md)): a thirteen-year-old at the dock who hears the hill. Fen is the one who clocks, in ch 6, that no aerial reaches eleven kilometres over water.

## What he carries

- He said Juno's name to [Judge](judge.md) over an open channel on night one, and he will spend the book finding out what that cost.
- He ran the only generator on a dark island and lit his house up like a beacon. The Trace's light is Fen's guilt ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#three-imagery-systems-keep-all-three-alive)); by ch 6 "he had stopped seeing it," and a drone has seen it for him.
- He controls the morphine.
- He is the one who learns, from the radio, that the quarantine is right.
- He knows the ship is a quarantine from day 7 and lets the road cheer.
- Fuel: the generator runs 7 p.m. to 7 a.m. on about 18 litres; the day runs on a car battery and his father's boat inverter for four hours ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md); [13](../13-resources-and-roles.md)).

## Power

Stillness. He absorbs force: softens a fall, stops a punch dead, drops lightly from height. Alone he is nearly impossible to hurt, but can only release stored force slowly, as a heavy shove. If he fills up with nowhere to put it, it hurts. He stores Lyra's excess momentum as anchor power: the more she moves, the more he can hold. See [Lyra](lyra.md#the-twins-power-flow) for the loop.

When the power comes, his body remembers how to dance. Arrives month two to three, and nobody expects the dance ([03](../03-power-system.md)).

## In a fight, and on a Tuesday

Alone: absorbs anything that isn't a bullet, one stored shove, then he fills up and has to stop. With Lyra: the loop, the best fighters on the island. Daily: catches what falls, steadies the generator, holds a wall section while Tock welds. The road houses fight in month five is the first time anyone sees them together, and the secret ends there. He is also the Trace's only mechanic, learning from the Harbour's apprentices and paying in ice. See [07](../07-powers-in-practice.md). Cost when he doesn't pass it to Lyra: headaches, and he has to shove something hard.
