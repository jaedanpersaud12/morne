# Lonny Hale, 17

**Element: Earth. Depth tier 3.**

## Core fact

His family was broke before his father got steady work and they moved up the Trace. Nobody on the Trace is rich, but the Hales remember being poorer. He remembers going to bed hungry as a small kid. His friends never knew.

## Who he is

Six foot three and still growing. Fit, good-looking, everybody's boy. Loves food, loves games, not the deepest thinker, always in a good mood. Drives the Hales' pickup too fast with six in the tray. Carried his sleeping mother from the bathroom floor to the bed before he thought about whether he should; his father went down in the La-Z-Boy. Locked his eleven-year-old brother Bix in the house and came to Juno's anyway, and said it like a confession. By the count in ch 5, both his parents are in the rows at the Mosses', and he carried every one of those fifteen sleepers in himself.

## Inside

**Want:** Wren and her brother Pip safe and fed on the Trace, and Wren's mother not left to rot in a bed on the road. He chases it from the first morning: the door he can't knock on, the "yes" he gives without asking anyone, and then (per [09](../09-relationships.md#lonny-and-the-girl-on-the-road)) the food he carries down past the barricade when the Trace won't take her in.

**Need:** To say what he wants out loud and argue for it. He has never once argued with Juno, and she wishes he would. He also needs to be fed without being ashamed of it.

**Lie:** If he stays easy, cheerful and useful, nobody has to know what he needs, and the next meal is the only question worth asking. Wanting more than his share is what makes you a burden.

**Fear:** Being the mouth the Trace can't afford. The day someone in the kitchen says out loud how much he eats is the thing he has been bracing for since he was small. Under that, the wall closing with Wren on the wrong side of it.

**Wound:** Hunger as a small kid, before his father's steady work, kept from everyone. **PROPOSED** ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#11--lonny--replaces-the-current-ch-11-fen), ch 11 beat): he was five, the fridge was a light with nothing in it, and his mother said she wasn't hungry and he believed her for years.

**How he loves:** He shows up and carries. "I came," three times on Wren's step. He lifts the man out of the bath, holds the torch, puts a glass of water in Sol's hand without a word and keeps refilling it, leans his whole weight into a stranger's shoulders so Juno can set the arm. He says yes to the person in front of him and thinks about the cost later, or not at all.

**How it shows:** He eats when he's tired or frightened, dry cereal from the box, four tins of corned beef. When something lands his head comes up (Juno says "your girlfriend's street," ch 2). Scared, he sits on the floor with his back against the cupboards and puts his head between his knees. At the door that matters he stands with his hands at his sides and doesn't knock. His shrug comes out wrong, too big. When it's over he sits under the tank tap with his clothes on and lets it run over his head.

**Humour:** Deadpan, literal, good-natured. "Yes, Mummy." "I'm eating *near* it." "Vanessa." "Veronica." He is the group's favourite target and plays the part. When he laughs he laughs silently, shaking, trying not to wake the kid asleep on him.

**What breaks him:** The kitchen scene where someone says it out loud. The gate shut on Wren. Wren's mother dying in her bed on the road anyway, after everything he carried down ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#side-arcs-under-the-main-plot)). Bix, if the stray shot in the siege is his (proposed, [16](../16-open-questions.md)).

**Happy:** The ch 1 boy: the pickup, the beach, the cooler, food, games, everybody's boy. A good day is a full stomach, a job his body can do, and his friends making fun of him for eating.

**Secret:** The hunger, then and now. He also knows the twelve bags in ch 8 were Judge's gift, and keeps that for Juno; he picked the box up without a word ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#secrets-ledger-who-knows-what-by-chapter-8)). And he has said yes to Wren on the Trace's behalf and nobody has called it. **PROPOSED** (ch 11): he goes down the road alone at night to turn Wren's mother and doesn't tell Juno.

**Book one leaves him:** He leaves for the road in ch 17 and is off the page for nine chapters, keeping twelve houses alive with his hands. The Junction learns not to cross him. Wren's mother dies in her bed anyway. He comes back for Bix's funeral (ch 26, if Bix is the one) and never says what the road was like. Whether Wren comes inside after the siege is still open.

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** good cheer.
**Hides:** shame about hunger.
**The surprise:** grief that comes out physically. On the page so far that is the grave in ch 5: three hours with a pickaxe for Mr. Hale, a man with his surname and no relation, after which he sits under the tank tap fully clothed. (Approved palette wording is "digging his father's grave"; see Open questions.)

## Voice

- "I'm hungry *now*. It's a different thing." ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md))
- "Okay. Okay. That's. He's doing what we're doing." ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md))
- "I came." ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md))
- "Same name as me. We're not family. Never were. Everybody on the road used to make the joke. Somebody with the name should do it." ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md))
- "Yeah. That's the thing about Fen." ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md))

Short, plain, light island English. He asks the practical next question ("So who do we call?", "And then?") instead of saying what he feels, and when he is upset his sentences break off ("That's. He's"). He repeats the one thing he has ("I came"). He never makes a speech; the longest thing he says in eight chapters is the reason he should dig the grave.

## Role

The carrier. Water up the hill from the Hills streams. Bodies moved. The wall built. With Tock, every run past the barricade.

## Relationships

- Bix, 11: his brother, awake, furious at being locked in. Attaches to Tock. By ch 7 Bix hasn't left Tock's side in five days, and it is Bix, not Lonny, on the Hales' tank stand screaming about the ship. Lonny said the locked door like a confession in ch 2 and hasn't mentioned it since. If Bix dies in the siege (proposed), Lonny comes back from the road to bury him.
- [Tock](tock.md): partners. The muscle and the heat. Tock's humour is mostly about Lonny, and Lonny takes it. He fixes what Tock's hands can't. They laugh together at Mr. Hale's grave. When Lonny leaves for the road, Tock does the runs alone and it nearly kills him.
- [Sol](sol.md): two doors down; little sister energy, which she half likes. They walk the road together on the first morning. She sees his father's shape in his sleeping face and keeps it from him; after the ice he fills her glass four times without a word.
- [Juno](juno.md): he's the one who asks "and then?" He doesn't argue. She wishes he would. He walks her to the Harbour, holds the boy down for her, and carries her first secret up the hill. The proposed ch 11 is the first time he goes against her, at night, without telling her.
- Wren, 17: his girlfriend, on the road below the bend. Short, crying and then not, holding Pip on her hip. "You took a whole night." She put her forehead on his chest. He said yes to her coming up without asking anyone. What he feels is plain and total; what it costs is the gate question, which is never settled because of her. Months 3 to 6 he leaves to live on the road.
- Pip, 5: Wren's brother, too big to be held and held anyway.
- Old Mr. Hale (Barnaby), 90: no relation, the road's running joke. Lonny dug his grave because somebody with the name should.
- [Fen](fen.md): the siphoning partner. Lonny repeats what Fen heard (Rust's name) without asking how he heard it.
- [Kit](kit.md): **PROPOSED** ("Box"): he brings the mattress and the turning schedule down to the flat, is kind about it, and says "you're one of us." She doesn't believe him.
- Cass: **PROPOSED** (ch 11): Lonny is put on Cass's mother and is the one who finds her dead. Cass asks why six people decide for thirty, and Lonny has no answer because he agrees.

## Power

Raw strength and density. He can lift a car off someone. **Cost:** it burns enormous calories. In a world where food is power, the strongest guy is also the hungriest, and feeding him becomes a political question. See [13](../13-resources-and-roles.md#food). He eats for three, and the day someone says so out loud in the kitchen is a chapter. Nosebleeds; his knees, by year two ([07](../07-powers-in-practice.md)). Arrives week one to two, deniable ([03](../03-power-system.md#how-powers-arrive)): so far a boy who carries fifteen adults in an afternoon, digs clay for three hours and doesn't need the other end.

## Fault line

His girlfriend Wren lives on the road below the bend. When the Trace blocks its road, she is on the other side. Whether she gets inside, and what Lonny does if she doesn't, is a year-one arc. The chapters name her Wren ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md)); [16](../16-open-questions.md) and [supporting](supporting.md#the-road-below-the-bend) still list her name as open and should be updated.

Second fault line, Lonny vs. the food ledger: is the strongest kid worth what he eats? ([10](../10-conflicts.md#inside-the-group))

## In a fight, and on a Tuesday

Throws a person. Throws a car door. Hits don't move him; bullets would. Daily: two drums of water from the Hills at a time, cars into the wall, the generator lifted, graves dug. Month four he carries Lyra home from the ambush instead of the water. See [07](../07-powers-in-practice.md).

## The seed and the chapter (added 2026-11-02)

The hunger is seeded in every scene he's in through chapter 8 (cereal from the box, four tins of corned beef, cold rice from a pot). It is loud enough that a reader already expects his power to be about strength or hunger. Dial it back a third until the kitchen scene where someone says it out loud, so the reveal lands as confirmation, not fulfilment.

**He has no POV chapter in the first eight and needs one in movement 2.** He has carried fifteen adults, dug a grave for a man with his surname, and brought his girlfriend and her brother up a hill without asking the six. Nobody has been inside his head. See [17](../17-book-one-outline.md#proposed-insertions-2026-11-02) for the proposed chapter.

What's inside: the hunger he never told them about, the La-Z-Boy shape of his father that Sol decided not to mention, and the fact that he has said "yes" to Wren on the Trace's behalf and nobody has called it. The "and then?" he asks Juno is not simplicity. It is a boy who learned early that the next meal is the only question worth asking.

## Open questions

- The approved surprise says "digging his father's grave," but his father is asleep in the rows; the grave in ch 5 is old Mr. Hale's, no relation. Options: the wording means Mr. Hale as a stand-in father (same name, Lonny digs because "somebody with the name should"); or it is a later beat where his own father dies in the rows and Lonny digs again, making the proposed ch 11's "two graves, both dug by a Hale" three.
- Cold rice from a pot is in the seed list above but not in ch 1 to 8 as written. Was it cut, or is it still to come?
- Is he in the siege? [07](../07-powers-in-practice.md#the-four-fights-of-book-one) has Lonny holding the gap; [17](../17-book-one-outline.md#movement-4-the-turn-ch-2127-months-610) ch 26 has him gone and coming back to bury Bix. Options: he comes back on night two and is too late; or he is away for the whole siege and the gap is held by someone else.
- Who says it out loud in the kitchen, and when? Options: Cass, in the ch 11 slot, with the food ledger as his argument against the six; or Juno herself, doing the sums, which would cost her more.
- Who has been turning Wren's mother on the road since day one? Options: nobody, which is the cost of his ch 4 "yes" arriving in ch 11; or Wren goes down by day and Lonny doesn't know how bad it is.
- Why does Bix attach to Tock and not him? Options: Bix hasn't forgiven the locked door; or Lonny has been carrying sleepers all day and Tock is the one in the kitchen.
- Does Wren know about the hunger? Options: she is the only one who does, from before the Trace; or she finds out on the road, where he can't hide how much he eats.
