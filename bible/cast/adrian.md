# Adrian Castell, 17

**Antagonist. No power. Depth tier: antagonist (full file, no POV chapters ever).** Decided 2026-11-03.

## The one rule for writing him

He is the book's plain bad person. Judge is right about his numbers, the Gardener is right that nobody has to die, Cass is right about his mother. Adrian isn't right about anything. The reader is never asked to understand him: no sad backstory that explains him, no redemption, no chapter from his side. He's there so the grey people have something to look grey against, and so the Trace's kindness has a price.

## Who he was

The quarry belonged to his family. *Castell Aggregates* blasted the south hill twenty years ago and shut it six years ago ([06](../06-island.md)); the steel gate at the top of the Trace still has the name on it. His father moved on to concrete and land in town. They live in the big house on the ridge above Morne Bay, with a pool nobody swims in and a wall with glass set in the top.

Private school in town. Good-looking, good at talking to adults, and cruel to anyone who couldn't hurt him back, which was nearly everyone. He was never punished for anything, because the adults who could have stopped him wanted something from his father. He learned early that being liked by the right people means never having to be good.

He has never been hungry, never been told no and never done a job with his hands.

## What the Sleep did

It took away the only people who ever made him behave, and left him with everything else: the house, the name, the money that doesn't matter any more, and five older boys from school who were already used to doing what he said.

## What he wants

What other people built. He doesn't need the Trace: the ridge house has a full pantry and a tank. He wants the Trace because it works, because thirty kids made something out of nothing, and he can't stand that it isn't his. Underneath that, he wants people to thank him for letting them keep what they made.

**His line:** "This is my father's land. You can stay. I'm not unreasonable."

## Why no power

He's dangerous the old way. People do what he says because they always did: he talks like a grown-up, he's sure of himself and he never raises his voice. Judge leads two hundred kids because he feeds them. Adrian leads five because they're afraid of being on the wrong side of him. The powered kids of book one are scary because of what they can do. Adrian is scary because he can make the Trace's own rules work against it.

He has his father's pistol and doesn't need to show it often.

## How the Trace answers him (and stays good)

The Trace never becomes him to beat him. They don't kill him, starve him or burn him out. They put him out of the gate the way you'd put out a dog that bit a child: firmly, once, without hate. They keep taking in the sick after him. They just check now, and checking costs them something every time (a kid who waits at the gate an hour with a fever because somebody has to look at her first). That cost is Adrian's real damage: not the medicine, the innocence.

## Relationships

- **[Juno](juno.md):** she stitched his leg. He calls her "Doctor" and means it as an insult she doesn't hear until ch 17. The person she helped without counting is the person who counted everything.
- **[Kit](kit.md):** the mirror. Both were let in. Kit was the new girl for eight months and never took a thing; Adrian was the new boy for two months and took everything he could carry. Kit sees what he is first, because she knows what it looks like to be grateful, and he isn't.
- **[Cass](supporting.md#quarry-trace):** Adrian uses Cass's grievance and Cass refuses him. Whatever Cass becomes, he was never Adrian's. That refusal protects Cass's greyness for the rest of the book.
- **[Judge](judge.md):** contempt both ways. Judge feeds people and Adrian has never fed anyone. Judge uses him once and is ashamed of it.
- **[Lonny](lonny.md):** the boy who grew up hungry and the boy who never was. Lonny is the one who wants to hit him and the one who doesn't.
- **[Tock](tock.md):** Adrian's father's gate is the one Tock cuts down and welds across the bend. By ch 24 the name on it is upside down.

## Voice

Polite. Full sentences, no dialect, an adult's vocabulary, the way he was taught to speak to his father's friends. He thanks people a lot. He never swears. He uses first names he hasn't been given. The politeness is the threat.

## Open questions

- What happened to his mother (abroad, asleep, or left years ago). Not needed for book one.
- Do his five boys have names? Give one of them a face (the one who hesitates) so the reader sees a boy, not a crew.
- Book two: Adrian and the Gardener, or Adrian and Rust.
