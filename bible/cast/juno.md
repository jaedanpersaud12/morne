# Juno Vale, 17

**Main character. Element: none through book one; Life/decay at the age line. Depth tier 1.**

## Who she is

Top of her class, top of the country, scholarship lined up. Everybody knows she is brilliant; there was a picture in the paper with a certificate and she drew a moustache on it and stuck it on the fridge. Kit saw the same picture in a barbershop in town before she ever knew Juno's face ([ch 1](../../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md)). Silly, warm and quick with her friends, because that is who she is. Tall, hair in a puff, wire glasses she pushes up with one finger. Wants to be a doctor. That was settled.

She is funny because she is funny. Not because of anything.

## The weight

Oldest of three, raised by a strict, devout grandmother ([Dot](supporting.md#quarry-trace)) after their mother died and their father went to Canada. Juno does the most, tries the hardest, and is still Granny's least favourite. Nothing is ever enough. When the scholarship letter came in August, Granny read it twice at the kitchen table with her glasses on and said: "So. That's done. Now you have to keep it." ([ch 2](../../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md))

Two years ago that weight brought her to a suicide attempt. She has lived with depression since, quietly, while still being the funniest person in the room. Both are true at once. In the worst stretch of that year she hid in the school library from lunch periods she couldn't face and read a nursing textbook nobody had checked out since 1998, cover to cover, "because reading still worked when nothing else did" ([ch 2](../../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md)). That book is now the Trace's medicine. She has rules for herself (thirty seconds, then up, then the tap, then the mirror) that she learned the hard way: if you let the shaking have as long as it wants, it takes everything.

Writing rule: never method, never a twist, never a cure. See [14](../14-writing-rules.md#writing-suicide-and-depression).

## In the story

The Sleep erases the future she worked her whole life for. Granny is asleep, so the approval she chased can never come now. She takes over the sleepers' care, studying her textbooks by candlelight, and becomes the Trace's medic. Not its leader: nobody is. The six run the Trace by domain (see [08](../08-factions-and-politics.md#leadership-inside-the-Trace)), and hers is the sleepers. But everyone outside treats her as the one in charge, and she keeps trying to carry it, because being enough is the only way she knows how to be loved. And she is the oldest, so she is first to approach the Sleep age: the future doctor racing a disease nobody understands, in her own body.

**Pressure:** Judge, Cass, the kids at the bend, all of them treat her as the leader. She is not. The friends keep refusing to let her be. That refusal is the book's spine: the ecosystem versus the one girl trying to be the whole forest.

## Inside

**Want:** keep Granny alive. Not the thirty-one. Her. If Granny lives, Juno gets to be enough. On the page it is water: the last of Dr. Moss's six bags goes into Granny ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md)), and when the numbers put Granny's name first on the next list, Juno looks down the hill at the hospital store room eleven kilometres away.

**Need:** to be carried. To let the friends take the weight she keeps picking up, and to believe she is loved when she is not useful.

**Lie:** she only matters if she is useful, and she has to do it alone. Granny taught her the first half; she taught herself the second.

**Fear:** that she is choosing wrong and calling it the book. Kneeling on Dr. Moss's floor she cannot tell whether she is obeying the rule or the answer she wanted ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L47). Under that, the old one: that if she stops for longer than thirty seconds, she won't get up.

**Wound:** a grandmother whose love had to be earned and never was, a mother dead, a father in Toronto whose voicemail she hangs up on before the beep ([ch 2](../../chapters/ch02-count/LATEST.md) L27). And the attempt, two years ago, which she came back from and still lives beside.

**How they love:** by doing the job for you. She asks Kit to the beach every time and means it every time. She waited out every one of Sol's silences for eleven years and sat on the end of Sol's bed through all of Form Two without saying a word ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L227). She hits Tock in the arm. She sits up with a ninety-year-old neighbour with a teaspoon because she was the one who decided he'd get the spoon instead of the bag.

**How it shows:** when she is in charge her voice goes fast and steady; her steadiest face only ever means something is badly wrong ([ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L171). She says "okay" in threes. She does arithmetic out loud to keep her hands still, smooths tape twice more than it needs, sits with one hand flat on the page and doesn't write. Her hand opens and closes at her side when she is told something she can't use ([ch 6](../../chapters/ch06-sixteen/LATEST.md) L133). She shakes in private, for thirty seconds, on the edge of a bathtub.

**Humour:** dry, fast, and aimed at the person she loves most in the room. "Tock, the goat," with Granny on the floor next door. "She's too rude to die. God doesn't want her." "The paper was a photo." "Don't climb walls," to a boy thanking her for setting his arm. The jokes get flatter and quicker the worse things are; she is still making them on day eight.

**What breaks them:** effort that wasn't enough. Mr. Hale got more water from her own hands than anyone in the ward and died anyway, and the same arithmetic then puts Granny first ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L183). Each bed death in book one is that again, louder.

**Happy:** loud. Head back, laughing harder than anyone at a joke her brother made ([ch 1](../../chapters/ch01-dust/LATEST.md) L47). The beach, the cooler, the moustache on the newspaper photo. The girl who walked across the Finches' yard at five to ask if Sol wanted to see a frog.

**Secret:** the twelve bags of IV fluid in the Mosses' front room were a gift from Judge, and she knows exactly what a gift from Cole Mayers is ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) L189). Only Lonny was there. Older and quieter: the thirty seconds in the bathroom, which nobody sees.

**Book one leaves them:** Granny failing after a year of Juno's hands. Juno carries her to the garden at night alone and can't, and lets Tock take the other end; Kit finds them there ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#movement-5-the-garden-ch-2830-months-1012), ch 29). Letting herself be carried, once, by the people who were always going to carry her. On her birthday she wakes up and is not asleep. Her hands are cold and she doesn't know why (ch 30).

## Emotional palette

**Shows most:** duty, dread.
**Hides:** wanting to be held.
**The surprise:** envy. The island chose Kit, and Juno has no power. Approved 2026-11-02; **PROPOSED** for the page, because no chapter shows it yet (Kit's power first shows in ch 9). The chapters already lean toward it: Juno meets every power so far as a resource ("Okay. That's one thing," over Sol's ice, [ch 4](../../chapters/ch04-ice/LATEST.md) L229; "How hot can you get it?", [ch 7](../../chapters/ch07-heat/LATEST.md) L225), never as something she might have had.

- "I can't do anything." (answering Judge's "You can't do nothing", [ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) L97)
- *this is the first thing I have ever done that I cannot undo.* ([ch 8](../../chapters/ch08-market/LATEST.md) L69)

Close third. Lists. Counting. Arithmetic as a way to keep her hands still. When she is in charge the sentences get short and flat; when she is alone they get long and circle back on themselves. She says "okay" three times when she is not okay; Lyra catches it off her ("Okay. Okay. Okay," the way Juno said it, [ch 3](../../chapters/ch03-signal/LATEST.md) L165). She carries the least dialect on the hill, because she has been correcting herself for a scholarship since Form One ([14](../14-writing-rules.md#dialect-is-class)): light island English with the friends ("We going down the beach"), standard the moment she is on duty or in front of the Harbour. She uses the book's words (deep coma, the bevel up) and circles the ones she won't say.

## Relationships

- **[Granny Dot](supporting.md#quarry-trace):** the whole want. Juno has spent her life earning Granny's approval and got "Now you have to keep it." Asleep, Granny is still the standard: her cough on the spoon is "any morning," her prayer is one Juno knows every word of and can't say ([ch 5](../../chapters/ch05-spoon/LATEST.md) L125). Over book one the bag, the spoon and the list all bend toward her, until Juno has to carry her to the garden and can't do it alone.
- **[Tock](tock.md):** her brother. She hits him in the arm and laughs harder than anyone. She is the only person who can talk him down. The feeling is the oldest one she has: exasperated, total. It costs something in ch 7, when she meets his fire with "how hot" instead of "are you safe", and he becomes a resource before he's a brother ([17](../17-book-one-outline.md#cost-audit-for-ch-1-to-8-what-each-turn-cost-and-whether-the-bill-has-come)). In the morning he says "Come back" and she says "Obviously," and he doesn't smile. Never breaks; he is the one who takes the other end of Granny in ch 29, and the one in the room at the age line.
- **[Olli](olli.md):** half a mother to him. He believes anything she says in the bored voice, and she uses it on him the first night ("Granny was sleeping hard"). She gives him real work (the eyes) because he asked for a job. Her lie to him about Granny is the one she will have to unsay first.
- **[Fen](fen.md):** both have been to the edge. They recognise it in each other without saying it. The quietest, deepest bond in the book. Not romance; he is the one person she doesn't perform for. Book one strains it twice: he gave her name to Judge on night one, and he kept the ship from her while she told the road someone was coming. She finds out from Judge, not from him (ch 10 at the latest), and that is the trust break. It mends when he tells her first what the radio says about the quarantine (ch 22). See [09](../09-relationships.md#juno-and-fen-the-edge).
- **[Kit](kit.md):** the only one who ever said Kit's name first ("Morning, Kit"), for the eight months Kit has lived at the bend; the house Kit ran to on night one. In ch 5 Juno is too tired to walk down and ask why Ambrose stays on the gallery; in ch 8 she looks past Kit at the steel box and keeps walking. Their fight over the sleepers is the Trace's central argument: Kit roots Ambrose (ch 9), it works, and it makes Juno's medicine look like cruelty. Neither is wrong; they stop speaking (ch 10). It mends with an act, in the garden, ch 29. Under it, from ch 9 on, the approved envy (**PROPOSED** for the page).
- **[Sol](sol.md):** eleven years since the frog. Juno has never asked Sol a question before she was ready, and doesn't ask what else the ice can do. That patience is about to be tested: Judge knows about the ice, and Juno has told Sol nothing ([19](../19-imagery-and-threads.md#secrets-ledger-who-knows-what-by-chapter-8)).
- **[Lonny](lonny.md):** the hands she leans on. He holds the torch for the spoon test, carries the box from the Harbour, and says "Yes, Mummy" to her orders. She wishes he would argue with her about Granny and he doesn't. He is the only one who knows where the twelve bags came from.
- **[Judge](judge.md):** wants her. The doctor from the paper. First as an asset, then as a rival, then as something harder to name. In ch 8 he is the first person who needs what she knows and says so ("You're what we got"), and the only person on the island who says numbers the way she does: flat, without hoping. He never lies to her; everyone else manages her. She takes his gift knowing it is a hook. See [09](../09-relationships.md#juno-and-judge-enemies-to-something).
- **[The Gardener](gardener.md):** offers her Granny's voice. The book's worst temptation. What he says to her through the bars in ch 28 is that nobody has to die.

## Her power (locked)

**None in book one.** She is the most valuable person in the Trace and she has no power. That is the theme in one fact, and the age clock is terrifying because of it.

**At the age line, she awakens instead of sleeping.** The roots follow the deepest groove, and her deepest groove is the night she tried to die. What grows there is Life/decay with the standard cost: her own lifespan. She can keep anyone alive by spending herself.

That is her lie made literal. The girl who believes she only matters if she is useful gets the one power that lets her be useful to death. She lives, and the thing that saved her is the thing that will kill her. Book two is her friends refusing to let her use it, and the Gardener telling her she never has to stop.

**When:** the last page of book one or the first of book two. The age line falls on her birthday, which is [open](../16-open-questions.md) but lands in the second bloom window or just before it.

**Rules for writing it:** no hint of it before the siege. No glow, no sign. The first use is an accident: a hand on a dying kid, and the kid lives, and Juno is grey for a week. Nobody, including her, knows what it cost until Kit tells her what the ground said.

- Ch 2 says she found the nursing book "two years ago" (she'd have been 15); ch 5 says she read it "at fourteen". Which is canon: change ch 5 to fifteen, or ch 2 to "three years ago"?
- When does Juno first say the twelve bags came from Judge? Option A: only when the second gift arrives with a price (current ledger). Option B: Lonny lets it slip first, and she has to explain why she kept it.
- Does she call Toronto again? Option A: never in book one; the father's voicemail stays unanswered until the scientist says his name in ch 22. Option B: once, when the network is dead, and she says the thing she didn't have words for on night one to a phone that can't carry it.
